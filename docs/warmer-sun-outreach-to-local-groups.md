# From cold outreach to a hub of local groups

A working brief from the Warmer Sun thread  
September 2026

This document is a synthesis of the conversation, not a transcript. It keeps the analysis, the mechanisms, the caveats on rates, and the recommendations that follow from them.

---

## 1. The question

Cold outreach is exhausted as a way to build a network and do business development.

- Email: most messages never get a reply. A 1-in-1,000 interested response is a realistic experience for generic volume.
- LinkedIn: paying Premium buys the right to message strangers — still interruption, still low reply.
- Reddit: moderators enforce no-spam / no self-promo. Ads buy attention. They do not buy standing.
- X: small accounts talk to small accounts. Large accounts do not owe a DM a reply. X Chat is usually a shout into the abyss.

In-person is the format that still works. It is also local. So the thread asked, in order:

1. What actually works for network and bizdev outreach?
2. What is the digital equivalent of being in the same room?
3. Do online community platforms differ in any substantial way?
4. Given that, how does Warmer Sun get people to self-host for fun, host for others, buy a commercial license, learn in a structured way, with warmersun.com as the hub and small city groups as the rooms?

---

## 2. What the numbers actually say

Treat every published “reply rate” as directional. Vendors inflate the high end. Studies mix “any reply” with “interested human,” and “per email sent” with “per unique contact.” The pattern is still brutal.

### Cold email

- Platform averages in 2026 often land around 3–4% any-reply (Instantly and similar sequencer datasets cite ~3.43%).
- A 2M-email study (Sales.co) found **2.09% any reply**. Of those replies, about 14% were positive, ~30% negative, ~45% auto-reply. Interested contact is closer to **0.6%** (~1 in 157).
- Some strict net-new programs print under 0.5%. Belkins’ 2025 net-new set was cited around 0.45%.
- Meeting rates are worse: one large set needed on the order of 300+ emails per booked meeting.
- The long collapse is real. One series goes from ~22% in 2010 to ~9% in 2023 to ~2% in 2026.

“One in a thousand” is pessimistic versus elite, tightly targeted campaigns (those still report high-single-digit to low-teens any-reply). It is realistic for generic volume. The user’s experience is not a personal failure. It is what the channel does now.

### LinkedIn

Recruiter InMail averages are not sales averages. LinkedIn’s oft-cited ~18% figure is recruiter-skewed and old. Generic connection notes and sales InMail in crowded verticals sit in the mid-single digits to low teens. Connection-plus-template-DM is still interruption. Volume caps (~100 connection requests/week, limited InMail) are the real constraint. Paying for the privilege of messaging strangers buys a more expensive ignore.

### X DMs

This was the best-supported complaint in the original question. A large 2026 production set of initial DMs averaged well under 1%. Small senders (1–3 accounts) do better than mass ops; following-then-DMing beats cold DMs. A ~400-follower account still lands in Message Requests, which most people never open. Vendor blogs claiming 8–15% “well-run X DMs” should not be treated as this dataset.

### Warm vs cold

Warm introductions, referrals, and already-known conversations convert an order of magnitude better than interruption, and they close faster. Vendor blogs love “40–60% meeting conversion” and “17x.” Treat the exact multiples as marketed. The durable finding is the *gap*, not a law.

Older circulating stats to avoid presenting as current fact:

- “84% of B2B buyers start with a referral” — Influitive ~2015, cited by HBR in 2016.
- “14.6% inbound close vs 1.7% outbound” — HubSpot ~2012 lineage. Direction holds. The precise multiple is fossilized marketing.
- “Events convert 40% of leads to opportunities” — often collapses meeting-to-opportunity with lead-to-opportunity. HockeyStack-style 2025 data showed a modest efficiency edge for events, not a 40% universal.

### What is well-supported as “the solution”

There is no remaining high-volume interruption channel with healthy unit economics for a small sender. That is the honest headline.

What still works is a change in *temperature*, not a new pipe:

1. Mine the graph you already have (specific double-opt-in intros).
2. Become recognizable before the ask (public work).
3. Inhabit one or two rooms for months with no ask.
4. Host more than you hunt.
5. Use IRL / small rooms as the densifier.
6. If anything unsolicited is sent: live signal, one specific observation, one question, existing public proof, lists of tens not thousands.

## 3. The mechanism (why every channel died the same way)

The channel is not the variable. The causal structure is.

Cold outreach fails on email, LinkedIn, Reddit, and X for the same reason: an unsolicited claim on scarce attention, with no prior evidence you are worth the 30 seconds. The recipient’s expected value of opening or replying is near zero. The cost is real (time plus the risk of more spam). When sending became cheap — sequencers, then AI — message volume exploded. Attention did not. Reply rates fell. That is mechanical, not a copywriting problem.

You can vary subject lines, platforms, personalization tokens, and paid InMail privileges and the prediction stays the same: low reply. Those tactics are easy to vary. They are bad explanations.

A stranger engages when at least one of these is already true:

1. **Trust was transferred.** Someone they already trust vouched. Warm intro / referral.
2. **They already saw you do the work.** Public artifact, product, comment, essay. Content-warmed, not cold.
3. **You share a high-bandwidth context.** Same room, same small community, same live problem.
4. **They moved first, or a live signal made the timing non-random.** Inbound, or a real trigger named in the first line.
5. **You already gave them something useful with no invoice attached.** Reciprocity exists before the ask.

Swap any of those for “sharper copy to the same cold list” and you are back at 1–3% replies.

### Compounding vs decay

Cold interruption is a decaying asset. Reply rates have been roughly halving every five to seven years as send volume exploded. Each new channel — email, LinkedIn, multi-touch, AI agents — saturates faster than the last.

Content, relationships, and hosted rooms compound the other way. Each artifact and each real tie raises the odds of the next one. The scarce resource is not messages, and not even content. It is trusted standing in a specific room.

### Questions the original framing hid

- What are you actually trying to get — customers, distribution, collaborators, talent, or capital? Those are different motions.
- What is the offer, and who already wants that specific next step?
- Who already knows the work? Unused inventory is usually larger than it feels.
- Why would a stranger spend 30 seconds on you *before* the message?
- Are you hunting or hosting?

Outbound still has a place: learning which offer resonates, reaching people who will never search for the category, covering the months before inbound compounds. Used that way, it is a scalpel on a named list of 20–50. Used as a volume machine, it is the 1-in-N job, and the unit economics get worse every year the models get cheaper.

## 4. What works on the platforms that were named

**Email.** Still usable as a pipe after warmth exists. Lukewarm follow-ups to people who already know the work can work. Spray from a warmed domain to a purchased list is the 1-in-N game. Separate domain, warmup, bounce under ~2% is hygiene, not strategy.

**LinkedIn.** Public surface and mutual-intro finder, not a paid spam cannon. Comment on the actual posts of named people for two weeks; then the connection request is not cold. Personal profiles outperform company pages. Depth beats a 10,000-connection junk graph.

**Reddit.** You cannot buy standing. Spend 90 days being useful in one sub. Then you have permission ads never buy.

**X.** Stop treating DMs as the motion. For a small account the working game is a reply-ladder: 20–50 accounts your buyers already read, substantive replies for weeks, then a DM after they have seen the name. Public replies are the warmup. Personal account, not brand account. Route recognition to something durable (email list, product, a hosted room). Followers are not pipeline.

**In person.** Shared physical context cannot be AI-spammed cheaply. A well-run small room produces denser conversations than a quarter of cold email. Do not believe “40% of event leads become opportunities” as a universal. Pre-booked meetings and small rooms convert. Booth scans and unfollowed-up badges do not. Most event programs die in the 48 hours after.

## 5. The digital equivalent of being in the same room

There is no full digital equivalent.

IRL works because several scarce things happen at once: simultaneous attention, an identity that is expensive to fake, unplanned repeats, high bandwidth, and a social cost to walking out. Most “virtual networking” copies the calendar invite and drops the rest. That is why a 40-person Zoom mixer feels like a cargo-cult dinner.

A 2024–25 comparison of scientific conferences (PNAS Nexus) found in-person events connected previously unacquainted pairs at about **40%**, virtual at about **22%**. Formal sessions worked in both modes. The hallway is what died online.

So the digital move is not “find the platform that replaces the room.” It is to rebuild one or two of those properties on purpose, then accept that trust still accumulates slower on a screen.

### What you are actually trying to copy

- **A third thing.** At a dinner you talk about the food, the talk, the city. The pitch is not the reason you are both there.
- **Repetition with the same faces.** One conference badge is a transaction. The third Tuesday with the same eight people is a scene.
- **Mutual selection.** Travel, a ticket, or an invite filters for intent. Anyone-with-a-link does not.
- **Bandwidth.** Face, tone, tangents, the extra twenty minutes after the official part ends.
- **Hosting vs hunting.** People who chose to show up are already warmer than people you interrupted.

### Formats that actually copy a property

1. **A recurring small live table.** Four to twelve people, cameras on, same host or same cast, weekly or biweekly, something to *do* besides introduce yourselves. Office hours, inventing nights, a salon, a coworking block, a 90-minute Zoom dinner with a facilitator. Closest analog of being in the same room. Size is load-bearing. Cross 15 and you have a webinar. Do it once and you have a meeting. Do it twelve times and you have a scene.

2. **A persistent inhabited room of tens to low hundreds of actives.** Serious Slack, a tight Discord, a Circle, an alumni group — the online café you keep walking into. Standing is earned by being useful for months. Selection is the product. A 10,000-member server with three people talking is a ghost town with a logo.

3. **Work as the room.** Co-create instead of “connecting.” Guest on their show; invite them onto yours; pair on a doc; ship a small collab; be the regular in someone else’s live room. Shared output is shared context. A 40-minute interview inverts the ask. Practitioner numbers on podcast guesting (guest-to-client or referral in the ~10% range over a year) are vendor-adjacent; the mechanism is real.

4. **Public work as the hallway.** Writing, building, and replying in public so distant people can bump into you without a DM. This is not a relationship. It is how the first private message stops being cold, and how strangers self-select into the live table.

Hosting still beats hunting online. You run the table. They chose to come.

### What is not the equivalent

Webinars and 40-person “virtual mixers.” Spatial-avatar conference halls nobody returns to. LinkedIn comment theater and engagement pods. Lurk-only Discords. Random-match virtual coffee at scale. A paid community you never speak in.

Limits you should not paper over: trust still forms slower on video; time zones are the new locality; cameras-off plus Slack-on-the-side destroy the bandwidth you came for; a follower is not in the room with you.

### The system that replaces “only local”

Digital rooms build a named graph across cities. Two to four trips a year to wherever that graph already clusters turn warm-online into IRL density. Digital is how you arrive already known. IRL is still the densifier.

Practically:

- Pick **one** existing room where your people already are and inhabit it for 90 days with no ask.
- Host **one** recurring live table of 6–12, same slot, a real third thing.
- Publish enough that a stranger can check the work in two minutes before they join.
- When two or three of those people land in the same city, collapse it into a dinner.

The scarce digital resource is not reach. It is a small room that exists next Tuesday, with people who chose to be there.

## 6. Do community platforms differ?

Yes — between *classes* of platform. Almost no, between brands inside a class.

Circle vs Mighty vs Heartbeat vs Skool is a vendor argument. Discord vs Slack vs WhatsApp vs a forum vs a feed is a physics argument. The software does not create the room. A few properties of the software change which IRL traits you can copy, and which you have to fight.

### Load-bearing differences

**1. Habitat vs destination.**  
People already open Slack, Discord, WhatsApp, or iMessage every day. They do not already open your Circle. A sloppy Discord people already have installed will beat a beautiful empty membership site. Most “owned community” migrations stall on this, not on missing gamification.

**2. Default social physics.**

- Discord: drop-in voice plus hanging out. Closest copy of “same room” and unplanned collision.
- Slack: work-mode text plus huddles. Closest copy of a professional hallway. Search is better. The vibe is a second job.
- Circle / Skool / Mighty / Discourse: async archives — bulletin board plus library. Good for standing that compounds. Bad as a dinner.
- WhatsApp: obligation-to-reply. Copies the group text *after* the dinner, not the dinner. Works at 8–20 people. Rots at 80, because every message hits every phone.
- Telegram: a megaphone that can also be a group. Fine for broadcast. Weak as a table.
- LinkedIn Groups and Facebook Groups: feeds. Hallway, not room.
- Reddit: a public hallway with anti-promo enforcement. You inhabit it. You do not host a sales table in it.

**3. Identity cost.**  
Slack and most paid memberships lean real-name / work-email. Harder to fake, better for bizdev trust, stiffer hang. Discord and Reddit lean handles. Easier to show up, weaker first-meeting trust with people who buy on reputation. WhatsApp is a phone number: high intimacy, high leak risk, terrible for strangers. That is why operator masterminds sit on Slack and inventing nights sit on Discord.

**4. Memory.**  
Chat evaporates. Discord search is weak. Slack’s free tier still has a short searchable window. A newcomer cannot see that you were useful six months ago. Forums, well-structured Circle spaces, and a public blog *do* compound. If the point is standing over time, chat is the wrong substrate. If the point is being in the room next Tuesday, chat is the right one.

**5. Native cost of entry.**  
Circle and Skool make paywall and application the product. That restores selection. Discord and Slack can be gated; most people do not bother, so free servers fill with tourists. Selection still matters more than the CMS. You can run an invite-only Discord of twelve.

**6. Who owns the graph.**  
On Discord and Slack you are renting. No email list, platform risk, and the room can vanish. On Circle plus a newsletter you have addresses. Substantial if you are building an asset. Irrelevant if you are inhabiting someone else’s room for 90 days.

### What is noise

White-label apps. Leaderboards. AI matchmaking. “Spaces” vs “channels” naming. Whether live video is native or a Zoom embed. Those change decoration, not same faces, a third thing, repetition, or cost of showing up.

Graveyards look the same on every platform: no weekly reason to arrive, too many rooms, founder as the only speaker, nobody in voice, newcomers ignored for a day. The platform did not kill them. The missing cadence did.

### Stack, not pick-one

1. Public hallway — X, a newsletter, a tight subreddit — so a stranger can check the work.
2. Habitat they already open — Discord if the people will hang and talk; Slack if they are operators who refuse Discord.
3. Recurring live table — Discord voice or Zoom. The software of the table matters less than the same eight people and a third thing.
4. Owned list — email — so a ban does not delete the graph.

For Warmer Sun specifically: inventing nights and friends rooms on Discord are the correct *class* for drop-in voice plus a third thing. Do not migrate to Circle unless a missing property is actually missing (ICP will not install Discord; you need a paywall as the filter; you need a durable archive beside voice; you need member emails).

Switch platforms when the culture or the body is wrong. Stay when the only problem is that next Tuesday’s table does not exist yet.

## 7. Warmer Sun: the desired end state

The product motion that follows from all of the above:

- People self-host for fun.
- People host for others.
- Schools, companies, and paid hosts buy a commercial license.
- People who want it can learn in a more formal, structured way.
- warmersun.com is the hub.
- They run small local community groups (see city meetups on the site).

This is not a “pick Discord vs Circle” problem. It is a federation: software + local tables + a license ladder.

### What already exists on the site and in the license

**Play is already the easiest door.**  
`warmersun.com/forge` — no account, no install, no card. Future Forge is inventing practice: a named place, a hex board, an AI co-inventor that advises while you lead. Cloud is optional (save progress, Continue, lessons).

**The ritual is already the unit.**  
Vancouver Invent Night: eight seats, two hours, one new technology, a real problem, Vancouver Public Library Central, Level 6 North, room 690. Beginners welcome. Free. Be there in the first ten minutes or the library can give the room away. Next date currently posted on Discord.

**The federation is already promised.**  
See if a Future Forge group exists near you. If not, register interest; approved groups appear as Coming… with a waiting list until they are solid. Vancouver is listed Active.

**The legal shape is already correct.**  
Future Forge is open source, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 for non-commercial use. Education is commercial use. COMMERCIAL.md (github.com/warmersun/future-forge) already grants the distribution you want:

- Free: host a meeting where you are present and participate, and do not charge for Future Forge.
- Free: run a free online server with no commercial intent and no charge.
- Paid license required: school, university, bootcamp, company, paid facilitation, selling seats, multi-tenant hosted offering.

Tiers contemplated: Education (annual seat or site license), Commercial (annual entity license), Cloud / managed service (annual minimum + revenue share or per-tenant). Request path today: email or GitHub issue titled “Commercial license.” Pricing is custom until public plans are published.

**Self-host exists, and it is a power-user path.**  
Get-started is Grok CLI, Node 22, `npm start`, optional Tailscale Funnel for friends anywhere. Friends install nothing; they join via browser with a room code. Friends consume the host’s Grok subscription. This is the right motion for “I want this on my machine for fun.” It is the wrong on-ramp for a teacher starting a Tuesday table.

**Structured learning already has a door.**  
Warmer Sun Cloud: lessons, lesson tutor, a name that follows you (quest log, Continue, achievements, inventor page). `/schools` states Future Forge is inventing practice for high-school seniors and up; a teacher can use it with a class; education needs a license. The page is thin. The path from meetup → lesson → license is not yet a handoff.

### What is missing

The four outcomes are four doors that do not yet hand off to each other.

- “Register interest” in a new city is not a form a stranger can complete in five minutes.
- The next Vancouver date lives on Discord, not on the hub.
- The free grant is almost invisible on the homepage. People cannot use a permission they cannot see.
- There is no Host Kit (agenda, cap, license one-liner, room-booking notes).
- There is little public artifact from nights already run (technology used, place invented for, a hold worth showing).
- Commercial buy path is a GitHub issue. Fine for the first three school deals. Fatal as a motion.
- Self-host looks like the path to hosting a group. If that stays true, only engineers host.

## 8. The design choice that decides whether this works

**Do not make self-host the path to a city group.**

A library night should run on the open table (`warmersun.com/forge`) or on one laptop in the room. Friends install nothing. Self-host stays the power-user and on-prem path. Host-for-others, at first, means you brought a ritual and a URL, not a server.

If you invert that, only engineers host, and the map stays one city.

Do not loosen CC-NC to chase WordPress economics. WordPress and Discourse allow commercial self-host; you already chose a different shape, and it matches the product (education is the commercial buyer). Do not tighten the license so hosting a free night feels risky. That kills the free grant that is your distribution.

Do not copy Toastmasters’ charter paperwork or CrossFit’s affiliate extract this early. Steal parkrun’s lesson: same ritual, listed only when it actually runs. Steal D&D’s shape: play at home free, host for friends free, organized play has a kit and an official listing, the publisher sells the structured books.

## 9. Recommendations

### The flywheel, in one line

Play on the open table → run a free night from a kit → get listed on the hub → the people who need it for school or work buy a license.

Local groups are the product. The site is the index and the kit. The license is the exception path, not the homepage.

### How the four outcomes connect

| They want | The door | What you give them | When money appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-host for fun | `/future-forge/get-started` | Own machine, friends on Wi-Fi, free server | Never, by design |
| Host for others | Host Kit + `/cities` | Ritual + listing + Discord hallway | Never, if they are present and not charging |
| Learn formally | Cloud lessons, then a cohort | Tutor, catalog, a name that follows them | After they have played |
| Commercial license | `/schools` + COMMERCIAL.md | Seats or site license, facilitator notes | When a school, company, or paid host needs cover |

The website is the hub in three jobs only: open table, city index, kit and license in plain language. Discord is the scheduling hallway, not the source of truth for dates. X and the essays are how distant people bump into the work. The local eight-seat night is the thing that creates the next host.

### This month — make Vancouver copyable

For a solo founder with tens of paying subscribers, not a chapter operating system.

1. **A real `/cities` page.**  
   Vancouver: Active, next date on the page (Discord can still be the reminder). Then Coming… and Start a table. The start form is the whole on-ramp: city, proposed cadence, venue type, email. You approve by hand. Ghost cities do not get a listing.

2. **A Host Kit, one page, not a training academy.**  
   Agenda for two hours. Cap of eight. One technology, one local problem. The ten-minute rule. How to book a library room. What to say on Discord. Attribution line. And the license in one sentence:

   > Friends night, you are in the room, no charge = free. School, company, or tickets = write us.

3. **Put that sentence on the homepage next to Invent Night.**  
   The free grant is your distribution.

4. **After every Vancouver night, put one artifact on the hub.**  
   The technology, the place they invented for, a hold worth showing. That is the public hallway so a stranger in another city can check the work before they start a table.

Do not launch a chapter OS, a franchise portal, Circle, or a train-the-trainer course. Cadence first.

### Next layer — after two or three tables besides Vancouver have met more than once

- Office hours for new hosts. Same format you already know: small live table, you host the hosts.
- Promote a city from Coming… to Active only after a second night with seats filled.
- Formal learning as a path *out of the room*, not the front door. One 4-week structured sequence in Cloud, pointed at from the kit: “if you want the tutor and the catalog after tonight.” Sell it to people who already invented, not to cold traffic.
- When a school asks twice, publish a ballpark Education SKU (site license vs seats). A number beats “open a GitHub issue.” Keep the issue/email path until then. Custom quotes are fine for deal one through three. They are fatal as the motion.

### Later

- Checkout for commercial.
- An “official table” badge so the hub stays the index of rooms.
- A one-click installer. Until that exists, the group default is the hosted open table.
- Cloud / managed hosting as a product only when someone wants to sell seats. That is a different business than city groups.

### What will break it

- Listing cities before a second night exists → a map of ghosts.
- Requiring self-host to host → a hobby for people who already ship software.
- Selling structured learning before the scene exists → a course with no room.
- Making the license feel like a trap around the free night → you kill your own distribution.
- Building the operating system for fifty cities while you still have one.

---

## 10. Conclusion

Cold outreach failed because interruption-without-context has negative expected value for the recipient when volume is free. Platforms were never the independent variable. Temperature was.

The digital equivalent of a local room is not a bigger app. It is a small recurring table with a third thing, plus a public hallway so strangers can check the work, plus an owned list so the graph survives a ban. Community platforms differ by class (voice hang vs work chat vs archive vs group text vs feed), not by vendor feature list.

Warmer Sun already designed the model that follows from that analysis: open table, free friends night, listed city groups, license for school and work, Cloud for structured learning. The work now is not a new vision and not a new platform. It is the handoff.

Prove the copy in Vancouver. Put the kit and the city form on the hub. Let the second and third tables appear from people who already sat in room 690 or invented on the open table. License the ones who cannot stay in the free grant.

That is the whole system.
