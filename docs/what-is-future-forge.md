# What is Future Forge

**Future Forge** is an **inventing practice**: you connect progress in emerging technologies to the world’s big problems — and invent a **pathway** that could make something scarce more abundant in a concrete place.

That *connection* is the point. People already hear about technology (even the Pope talks about AI). Hearing is not understanding. Understanding is being able to **connect the dots**: *what just became possible → what human problem it can touch → how, here.*

Warmer Sun exists so that map is available early — for a broad and young audience — as **the missing subject**, not as a job funnel. Future Forge is where the subject becomes practice. Site: [warmersun.com](https://warmersun.com).

## Learning goals

We want people to leave with a few deep habits of mind — not a certificate in a single tool.

### 1. Know what technology *is*

Most people do not have a sharp definition. Ours is practical:

> **Technology is what takes something that used to be scarce and figures out a way to make it abundant.**

Heat in a house is technology. Turning on the light is technology. Clothing is technology — think Ice Age needles sewing skins. Even **speech** is a technology: a tool that made shared meaning far more abundant than pointing and guessing.

The only force that has **consistently** improved the human condition across history is this kind of progress: scarcity loosened, again and again, for more people. Politics, culture, and ethics matter enormously — they steer *who* gets the abundance and *what* we do with it — but without the tool that multiplies capability, we stay in the old scarcity.

**Goal:** Learners can say what technology is, point to ordinary life as tech, and stop treating “technology” as only phones, apps, or AI.

### 2. Connect progress to global problems

News cycles, pulpits, and feeds talk about breakthroughs. The open question is:

- Can you **connect the dots** from a capability advance to a **grand challenge** — climate, health, energy, poverty, rights, long-term risk…?
- Can you invent a **pathway** — a build with emTechs that gets stronger as those technologies improve exponentially over time?

**Goal:** The inventing practice *is* that connection — repeated until it becomes a reflex.

### 3. Hold a broad view — not “just AI”

AI is one family. It is loud. It is not the whole map.

There are **eleven categories of emerging technology** (emTech). Future Forge’s tech tray and the **emTech Convergence Canvas** are built so that map is easy to **see, remember, and combine** — bits on one side, atoms on the other, bridges in between. Stacks reward fit to the problem, not “mix everything.”

**Goal:** Learners can name the landscape, not only the headline tool of the year.

### 4. Know what *emerging* means (and what it does not)

Not every technology is “emerging.” Not every technology advances **exponentially**. Why not?

- Many mature tools improve **linearly** or plateau for a long time (better processes, cheaper materials, wider access — still important, not the same curve).
- **Emerging** technologies are those still riding a steep capability curve: information-based and information-enabled families that compound — better tools make better tools — so progress can surprise you if you only watch last decade’s rate.
- Some domains look slow until a **convergence** unlock (storage + solar, AI + robots, networks + sensors).

**Timing** is not a slogan. The invention has to **intercept the exponential curve** — field the idea when the capability is real enough to help, early enough that the crisis has not already won. **Too early** is vapor: the tools cannot do the work yet. **Too late** and the place has already fallen. Timing literacy is knowing *which* curves are steep *now*, and where your invent sits on that rise.

**Goal:** Learners invent to intercept the curve — not too early, not too late.

### 5. Practice convergence and polymathy

Hard problems almost never sit inside one department. Pathways usually **cross** families: sensing + networks + energy + something with a body in the world, or a life-science step, or a coordination layer.

**Polymathy** here is not collecting trivia. It is **permission and skill to combine** domains when the place needs it — the Convergence Canvas made visual and playable.

**Goal:** Learners invent *across* the canvas, not only deeper into one silo.

### 6. Invent *pathways* — not static “solutions”

We deliberately say **pathway**, not solution — because of **time and the exponential curve**.

You solve a problem by **building with emTechs**. Those components are not frozen. As each emerging technology improves **exponentially**, every piece of the build gets better — cheaper, more capable, more accessible. The whole assemblage rides that rise: what starts as a local invent can **scale exponentially** as its ingredients compound.

That **future trajectory** is the click. A one-shot “solution” sounds finished on today’s tools. A **pathway** is a design that stays *on the curve*: start honest for now, then watch the stack strengthen year after year because the underlying emTechs keep improving.

| Word | Misses |
|------|--------|
| **Solution** | Fixed answer on today’s capability — no story of how exponential progress multiplies the whole thing over time. |
| **Pathway** | Build with emTechs so that as they get better, **the entire approach gets better** — the long arc of scale is part of the idea. |

**Goal:** Learners invent builds whose future gets stronger as emTech curves rise — not only a snapshot that works once.

### 7. Treat ideas as valuable — this is not STEM class

This practice is **not STEM** in the school-subject sense. STEM skills are wonderful when you build. Here the first craft is different.

Ray Kurzweil, in the preface to *Danielle: Chronicles of a Superheroine*, names the belief the inventing practice rests on:

> No matter what quandaries we face—business problems, health issues, relationship difficulties, the great social and cultural challenges of our time—there exists an idea that will enable us to prevail. We can and must find that idea. And when we find it, we need to implement it.

- **Ideas have value.** Naming a mechanism, a beneficiary, a timing claim, and a tradeoff *is* work.
- It is okay to **play pretend** — fantasize, imagine, brainstorm — when you are learning. Building a prototype is great. Implementation is hard and important when things are real. It is **perfectly okay to start with play and learning where it does not have to be real yet.**
- Values and ethics are in the room (challengers: systems, ethics, stakeholders, nature) — not bolted on after the “tech part.”

**Goal:** Learners take imagination seriously without confusing a brainstorm with a shipped system — and without needing a lab to begin.

### 8. Learn under voluntary attention (make it fun on purpose)

Is pre-calculus fun? Often not. People still do it because school **requires** it and pathways punish skipping it.

The missing subject is **not** mandatory. No standard exam. No diploma line. So the only way to scale learning is to make the practice **as fun as possible** — inventing under pressure, concrete places, friends, AI help on the fly — not a wall of “read first, invent later as a reward.”

**Goal:** People *choose* another hour because inventing is sticky — and they leave smarter about the map.

## The missing subject (context)

Exponential progress in emerging technologies reshapes every other field, yet it rarely appears as **general education**. You do not study chemistry only to become a chemist; you study it to understand the world. EmTech literacy belongs in that company — for high-school seniors, undergrads, and junior professionals — **literacy plus agency**, not only vocational training.

Warmer Sun follows the intellectual current of studying advancing technologies deliberately (including arguments such as Kurzweil’s [*A university for the coming singularity*](https://www.ted.com/talks/ray_kurzweil_a_university_for_the_coming_singularity), 2009), with a different center of gravity: **younger, broader, earlier**. Formats can change. The mission does not: curated substance + facilitated inventing practice.

## What you practice in a session

### Instantiating a global problem

A **Quest** usually takes a **global problem** and **instantiates** it: makes it concrete with a **place** and (fictive) **people** who live the harm. You invent for *them*, not for an abstract slogan about “the world.” Crisis pressure and a calendar still press — the place can fall if you wait too long.

### Building a pathway

You **invent**: stack emTech from the broad map; write **how it works** and **everyday life**. An **AI co-inventor** can teach, draft, and help check whether claims sit on the right part of the curve — **you lead**. The invent gate is never “AI invents for you.”

You also practice the **Progress / Prediction** side of the map: what emTechs can be used for, what the **trends** are, and projecting **predictions** — so the pathway is not only a story about today, but a trajectory that rides improving capability.

Claims face a feasibility light for *this year* and *this place* (categories stay pickable; over-claiming goes red). A hard question may still land (Moloch, Ethicist, Stakeholder, Mother Nature). Then you **field** it — pilot → scale — while the crisis can still win.

### Three perspectives on the invent (unless the Quest narrows them)

Unless a Quest turns meters off and focuses on one or two roles, the invention is judged from **three** angles:

| Perspective | What you practice |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Here and now** | Does it help *on the ground* — mitigate symptoms people feel today? |
| **Root cause** | Does it move the needle on the **underlying** problem, not only the surface? |
| **Support** | Can you earn **goodwill and buy-in** — public and policy support so the pathway can actually field? |

Local relief without a deeper lever is incomplete. A clever root-cause story with no support never leaves the notebook. Support without a real fix is theater. The three together are the inventing discipline.

### With others: compete, but learn cooperation

In multiplayer, learners can race personal invents — and still share **one** place and **one** crisis. If the challenge is not solved in time, **everybody loses**. So the practice is not only competition: it is learning that some stakes are shared, and **cooperation** is part of inventing well under pressure.

Optional paths: learning modules (tutor, one idea at a time), spotlight Quests on recent advances.

## Eleven families (the broad map)

The Convergence Canvas and Future Forge’s emTech categorization share the same intent: **eleven families** you can hold in your head — not an infinite news feed.

Historically (6Ps hex tiles) and in spirit today: **computing, clean energy, crypto, AI, robots, networks, transportation, VR/AR, IoT/sensors, 3D printing / fabrication / materials, synthetic biology / life tech** — with finer subtypes when you drill in. **Bits** (information side), **atoms** (physical world), **bridges** that span both.

AI is one seat at the table. Polymathy is pulling the right neighbors into a pathway.

## Where it came from

### The tabletop (2021)

Future Forge did not start as software. In **2021** Warmer Sun tried to build a **tabletop** inventing practice: board, dice, cards, and hexagonal **emTech** tiles — **6Ps – Pun Intended** (© 2021 Tamas Simon / Warmer Sun Education). Historical design notes: [`6Ps Game - Pun Intended - Hex emTech.pdf`](./6Ps%20Game%20-%20Pun%20Intended%20-%20Hex%20emTech.pdf).

The **6Ps** were the structure of the invent:

| P | Meaning |
|---|--------|
| **Progress** | What is going on *today* in science and technology |
| **Prediction** | Major milestones we estimate on future timelines |
| **Problem** | Something present to address now |
| **Prevention** | A future harm we still have time to prepare for |
| **Pathway** | What players invent — a build with emTechs that can ride exponential improvement |
| **Pun** | A nod to Diamandis’s **6Ds of exponentials** (digitized, deceptive, disruptive, demonetized, dematerialized, democratized) |

The table’s job was the same as today’s practice: connect emerging tech to global problems, invent pathways, semi-cooperative stakes (if existential threats go unhandled, everyone loses). Impact was counted in **I$** — Impact Dollars — after the idea that the best way to make a million dollars is to impact a million people.

It was **too early**. Not because the ideas were wrong — because the **blockers** were real.

### The contradiction at the center

The hard problem was never “how do we keep people busy with moves?” It was how to keep **invention** alive.

If the design **caters** to people — hands them everything ready so they only **make moves** — the mechanics take over. They do not need to think. They do not invent. Who would a designer be to script **how the world will look** years from now, card by card? Fixed futures lie, and they kill the learner’s work.

But if people truly **brainstorm**, another problem appears: **who wins?** Who decides whether an idea is feasible or good? Other people at the table? Early **voting** on ideas (an I-cut-you-choose style filter in 6Ps) felt immature as the sole judge of truth. Popularity is not feasibility. Applause is not intercepting the curve.

And most people simply **lacked knowledge of emTechs**. Without a map of categories, trends, and predictions, inventing at the table collapsed into guesswork or silence.

### Progress and Predictions — the literacy bridge that asked too much

That gap produced the introductory course **Progress and Predictions**, written and put online at **[101.warmersun.com](https://101.warmersun.com)**. The hope was straightforward: learn the missing subject first, then as a **reward** play the tabletop inventing practice.

In practice that funnel was **too much to ask**. A solid course is valuable. Making inventing contingent on finishing homework first is a wall, not a door — especially for a subject that is **not mandatory**. People will not climb a pre-calculus of exponentials for fun before they are allowed to invent.

### What AI unblocked

**AI** enabled a full rethink — not by inventing *for* the learner, but by removing the blockers that had made the tabletop too early:

| Blocker | What changed |
|---------|----------------|
| People don’t know enough emTech | Learn **on the fly** — primers, co-inventor, tutor modules |
| Hard to get help mid-invent | A partner that proposes; **you still lead** |
| Who judges “good” or feasible? | **Feasibility** and whether the invent **intercepts the curve** can be checked against capability and place — not only peer votes |
| Course-then-play funnel | Invent and learn in the **same session** |

No system — human or AI — gets to claim it knows the future. What *can* be asked is whether a claim is honest for **today’s capability** and whether the pathway is built so that as emTechs improve, the whole thing can get better. That is enough to protect **invention** without killing it with ready-made moves.

Future Forge is that rethink made concrete: the inventing practice for the missing subject, with the 6Ps still in the bones (progress, prediction, problem, prevention, pathway) and the contradiction still honored — **you invent; mechanics and AI serve the invent, not the other way around.**
