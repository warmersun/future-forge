# Play the breakthrough: Future Forge as a participatory companion to technology media

**Status:** Strategic proposal endorsed for documentation and evaluation; not a validated acquisition strategy or an implementation approval.  
**Starting conditions:** Zero users, no established prospects, no marketing budget, and untested customer assumptions. Sic already participates on X and attends local meetups. Online distribution is primary; Vancouver outreach is deliberately limited.  
**Scope:** Document the concept, compare it with Sponsored Quests, evaluate the broader format opportunity, and define a small test. No application, schema, access policy, licensing, or publishing changes are authorized by this document.

## 1. The strategic proposition

**Turn a technology story into something its audience can participate in.**

A writer, podcaster, researcher, or other creator explains a technology development. Future Forge contributes a concrete, source-grounded puzzle through which the audience explores its implications by inventing. The invitation is not “try our application” or “help us with customer discovery.” It is:

> You have read what this technology can do. What would you do with it?

“Play the breakthrough” is the initial campaign concept. The broader format is a **Future Forge puzzle**: an invitation to process technology news, ideas, opinions, and forecasts through active exploration rather than only consumption or commentary.

The long-term ambition is for an accompanying Future Forge puzzle to become a familiar expectation: an author publishes a consequential idea, and readers can explore what follows from it. This would make Future Forge a recurring way of engaging with technology media, not merely a destination people must remember to visit.

**Recommendation:** Pursue this as an editorial distribution experiment using the existing Quest foundation. Treat sponsorship as an optional funding relationship, not the defining identity of the format. Prove one useful collaboration and voluntary repeat participation before building a publishing platform.

## 2. What changes from the previous approach

The problem is not that Sic has failed to hear “engage online” or “attend meetups.” Those activities already happen. The proposed change is the exchange offered within those relationships.

| Previous invitation | Proposed invitation |
|---|---|
| A stranger is asked to try unfamiliar software and discover its value. | A reader is invited to explore a question arising from something they already care about. |
| A creator is asked to promote Future Forge. | A creator receives a finished, relevant extension of their own work. |
| Sic must assemble an audience for a new public event. | Participation begins in an existing publication or discussion, subject to its owner's permission. |
| Research participants give the founder their time as a favor. | Participants receive an experience intended to be worthwhile in its own right; follow-up research is transparent and optional. |

This does not eliminate recruitment. It gives a potential distributor and participant a more concrete reason to say yes. Whether either values the offer remains untested.

The route also avoids requiring a severe pain to justify a learning product. Curiosity, mastery, imaginative exploration, social discussion, and uncertainty about future choices are all possible motivations. We should learn which actually bring people back rather than choose a fear and attach the product to it.

## 3. The exchange: reader, publisher, and Future Forge

### The participant

**Hypothesis:** “I understand the claim well enough to repeat it, but I want to explore what it would mean in practice.”

The proposed benefit is a concrete opportunity to form an idea, examine its assumptions, and discover a consequence they had not noticed. It may be enjoyable or useful without resolving a career decision. Do not promise an AI-proof career, accurate prediction, implementation readiness, or discovery of a person's purpose.

### The editorial partner

**Hypothesis:** Some creators want their audience to do more with an explanation than agree, argue, or move on.

They receive a prepared companion activity, faithful attribution, and follow-through on participant contributions. Candidate benefits include better questions, thoughtful reader responses, and a reason to revisit the original piece. None is a proven engagement or retention improvement.

The partner supplies access to an existing audience, editorial context, and permission to publish the companion. They are not required to fund the first experiment, endorse Future Forge's judgements, or hand over an audience list.

### Future Forge

Future Forge contributes research, scenario design, facilitation where necessary, and a deeper inventing experience. In return it gets an opportunity to earn participants through useful work, plus evidence about the fit between the activity and the application.

The scarce initial resource is founder time. Zero marketing spend does not mean zero authoring, hosting, inference, moderation, or support cost.

## 4. The repeatable editorial format

A puzzle is an **open-ended, bounded inventing challenge**, not necessarily a quiz with one correct answer. Different approaches can be defensible. Rejecting an unsupported premise or recognizing that an idea is too early can be a useful result.

Each companion should contain:

1. **The source and its claim.** Credit and link to the original, identify the author and publication date, and distinguish quotation from our interpretation.
2. **The evidence status.** State what is demonstrated, inferred, proposed, disputed, or forecast. Show important limits and the review date.
3. **A concrete situation.** Give the reader a human problem in a fictive place, not a disguised product demonstration.
4. **An invitation to invent.** Ask what they would change or make possible. Preserve more than one reasonable approach.
5. **A consequential constraint.** Include the limitation, tradeoff, dependency, or timing issue that makes the thought worth doing.
6. **A contribution and reflection.** Let participants express their approach, explain an assumption, and say what changed in their understanding.
7. **A continuation.** Offer the corresponding Future Forge Quest when the participant wants deeper exploration; state the real access requirements.

These are editorial requirements, not newly defined JSON fields or a new runtime mode.

### Two depths, one idea

**First contact:** A short challenge can live in the partner's normal article, post, comments, or discussion. The reader can contribute a few sentences without opening a new application. This is a distribution prototype, not proof that the application is already frictionless or playable inside that surface.

**Deeper participation:** The corresponding Quest provides the board, capability context, AI co-inventor, timing, and challenges. The participant leads. The AI is a fallible advisor, not a certification authority.

The bridge must preserve the same question. Do not attract someone with a specific situation and then drop them into a general catalog or unrelated tutorial. Do not promise a short completion time before observing actual first-time participants.

If readers enjoy the first depth but never want the second, the result may support an editorial puzzle format without supporting the current application experience. That distinction is essential.

### Illustrative example: robots at a food bank

This is a format illustration, not a researched capability claim, existing Quest, or partner project.

- **Source context:** An explanation of a documented improvement in robotic manipulation.
- **Situation:** A small food bank receives unpredictable donations and has too few people to sort them.
- **Invitation:** Where would you apply the capability, and what would you leave to people?
- **Complication:** Packaging changes, damaged items need judgement, and space is limited.
- **Contribution:** Describe an approach and the assumption on which it depends.
- **Deeper exploration:** Develop that approach in a Quest whose grounding includes the verified capability limits.

The source establishes the actual capability. The fictive situation makes its implications explorable. Neither the exercise nor a favorable game judgement proves a real deployment would work.

## 5. News, ideas, opinions, and forecasts need different handling

“Play the breakthrough” is a useful entry phrase but too narrow for the entire ambition. Not every source describes a breakthrough, and not every claim deserves to be accepted as a premise.

| Input | What the participant explores | Editorial safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| **Technology news:** a reported result, release, or deployment. | What the demonstrated change could enable in a particular situation, and what remains blocked. | Distinguish a lab result from a field deployment, a vendor claim from independent evidence, and availability from affordability. |
| **An idea:** a proposed mechanism, combination, or application. | Whether they can construct a coherent approach using the idea and identify its dependencies. | Label the proposal as a proposal. A persuasive explanation is not evidence that the mechanism works. |
| **An opinion:** an interpretation of technology's consequences or desirability. | What changes if the argument's assumptions hold, and whether an alternative interpretation changes the invention. | Attribute the view; make assumptions and counterarguments visible. Disagreement with the author is not a failure condition. |
| **A forecast:** a dated claim about future capability, adoption, or cost. | What becomes possible if the forecast holds, what fails if it slips, and which observable milestone matters next. | Preserve attribution, date, uncertainty, and scope. Do not present the forecast as current capability or extrapolation as certainty. |

The existing engine need not support automatic paired scenarios or forecast tracking to test these editorial ideas. Manual companion notes can expose the alternatives first. New product behavior requires a separate proposal.

Not every item should become a puzzle. Reject thin speculation without an intelligible premise, stories that require copying protected material, and exercises whose only meaningful answer is “agree with the author” or “buy this product.” Purely normative disputes may need discussion rather than a capability-based inventing Quest.

## 6. Compare and contrast: Sponsored Quests

### The shared foundation

The existing Sponsored Quest concept already turns a real capability advance into a design challenge. Both approaches can use the same Quest format, fictive situation, capability grounding, optional tutoring, and human-led invention. Both should make the implications of technology concrete without forcing a particular vendor's product as the answer.

**The difference is primarily the relationship and route to participation—not a different game engine.**

| Dimension | Sponsored Quest offer | Editorial companion / Future Forge puzzle |
|---|---|---|
| Starting point | An organization wants people to understand and explore a capability or product-category advance. | A creator has published an explanation, idea, opinion, or forecast that invites exploration. |
| Primary counterpart | A sponsor or commissioning organization. | An author, editor, publication, podcaster, or community curator. |
| Initial exchange | A commercial arrangement can fund authoring, delivery, or participant access; the feature alone is not a sale. | Future Forge contributes a tailored activity; the partner agrees to a specific publication or discussion placement. |
| Audience source | Must be explicitly agreed. The sponsor may bring its own audience; Future Forge cannot sell an audience it does not have. | The first experiment specifically depends on the partner's existing audience and an agreed placement. |
| Participant invitation | Explore what this capability makes possible through a challenge. | Explore the implications of the particular thing you just read, watched, or heard. |
| Attribution | An actual sponsor is identified as sponsoring the experience. | The source author is credited; editorial collaboration is identified only when agreed. Source credit alone does not imply a partnership. |
| Editorial independence | Payment must not purchase favorable scoring, product-prescribed answers, or the omission of material limitations. | The exercise must not convert the author's opinion or forecast into authoritative truth. |
| Funding | Sponsorship can underwrite delivery and inference under agreed terms. | The pilot may be contributed labor with tightly bounded operating cost; later funding remains a separate decision. |
| Early evidence | A real commission, actual participation, and evidence the participant receives value. | A real editorial placement, actual contributions, movement into the Quest, and voluntary repeat interest. |
| Main failure risk | An advert dressed as learning, or a sponsor expecting reach that does not exist. | Bespoke unpaid editorial work that readers enjoy but that never produces sustained Future Forge use. |

### They are overlapping dimensions, not mutually exclusive product types

A puzzle can be independently authored, editorially co-produced, sponsored, or both co-produced and sponsored. A publisher, source author, funding sponsor, and Quest author may be different people or organizations.

Recommended conceptual separation:

- **Content:** What source, claim, and situation are being explored?
- **Editorial relationship:** Who created or approved this companion, and who distributes it?
- **Funding:** Who pays for authoring and participation, if anyone?
- **Access:** Who can participate, under which actual account, entitlement, hosting, and usage conditions?

Do not use `sponsorName` merely to credit an article's author. It displays “Sponsored by,” affects catalog classification, and is also consulted by a billing helper. Misusing it is both misleading and potentially behavior-changing.

The initial experiment should reuse the existing Quest model. It does not need a new `kind`, a new catalog tab, or a publisher dashboard. Source and collaboration disclosures can initially live in the visible companion and authored content, with any durable in-app attribution gap documented for later review.

### How they can reinforce one another

Editorial distribution could produce evidence that people actually participate. That evidence could later make an honest sponsorship offer stronger. A sponsor could then underwrite a publisher's puzzle series or participant access, while the publisher retains editorial independence.

The order matters: do not sell hypothetical future readership as present reach. A first sponsor who brings its own audience is a separate viable hypothesis, not something that requires pretending the editorial route has already worked.

## 7. Evaluation of the opportunity

### Why this is worth testing

- **It meets an existing moment of attention.** The participant has just encountered the source, so the puzzle does not need to manufacture interest in the subject from nothing.
- **It offers a reason to distribute.** The creator receives an inspectable contribution rather than a request to advertise unknown software.
- **It connects to the product's purpose.** Learning through invention is the activity, not a marketing gimmick unrelated to the application.
- **It makes the source of visitors explicit.** Distribution depends on a particular partner placement, not a hope that an empty account's post will spread.
- **It is repeatable in principle.** New sources can create new occasions for the same practice. Repetition still has to be earned.
- **It has an existing content foundation.** Quest authoring, grounding, packaging, and sponsorship attribution already exist in the repository.

These are reasons to run an experiment, not evidence of demand, cost efficiency, or competitive uniqueness.

### What could make it fail

| Risk | Practical response and evidence to seek |
|---|---|
| Creators do not want to divert readers or risk their credibility. | Offer a companion in their existing space, preserve source credit and a return link, and let them inspect it before publication. Record their actual objection rather than assume exposure is always valuable. |
| Readers want an answer, not an activity. | Make the first action small and concrete. Observe whether anyone contributes without being individually coached or obliged. |
| Readers like the challenge but find the application too demanding. | Measure the transition separately. Do not call comments or brief responses application adoption. |
| Every collaboration needs extensive bespoke work. | Track research, authoring, revision, moderation, and support time. Reuse the format, but never reuse factual grounding without checking it. |
| AI judgement appears to validate a dubious source. | Separate source claims from evidence, place critical limits in AI-visible grounding, and explain that a game outcome is not factual verification or professional advice. |
| The author expects an exercise that proves their thesis. | Make editorial independence explicit before agreeing. Decline work that requires predetermined agreement or sponsor-favored outcomes. |
| A no-budget experiment quietly creates an inference bill. | Agree a bounded access arrangement before publication. Stop or limit use according to actual capacity; do not advertise unlimited free play. |
| Success depends entirely on Sic facilitating every participant. | Treat guided sessions as onboarding evidence. Test whether participants and partners subsequently continue without that level of assistance. |

**Overall judgement:** This is a stronger strategic fit than generic promotional outreach because the distribution offer and the learning experience reinforce each other. Its weakest link is the unproven step from “interesting companion” to “I want to invent in Future Forge again.” The pilot must expose that step, not hide it behind flattering feedback.

## 8. Could it become a de-facto standard?

The ambition is plausible as a direction, but “standard” has three distinct meanings:

1. **A reader habit:** Encounter an interesting technology claim, then explore its implications through a puzzle.
2. **An editorial convention:** Publishers routinely commission or attach a Future Forge puzzle, as a recognizable companion format.
3. **A technical interchange format:** Independent authors and systems can produce and exchange compatible content.

The first two are the initial strategic goal. The existing portable Quest JSON and `.ffquest` package help with the third, but a file format alone does not produce an editorial convention or reader habit.

### Conditions for the ambition to become credible

- Participants recognize the activity and want another one across different sources.
- Publishers request a second companion because the first was worthwhile, not merely because it was free.
- Authors can reproduce the quality without Sic personally designing every detail.
- The experience is easy to enter from the original source, with access terms understood in advance.
- Source attribution, evidence status, correction history, and sponsorship are legible and trusted.
- Production and delivery costs support the frequency people want.
- Partners can understand permissions and commercial terms without negotiating an ambiguous arrangement every time.

Wider distribution could create a reinforcing loop: publishers introduce participants; useful participation encourages repeat publication; more authors create more relevant puzzles. That is a hypothesis about how growth might happen, not a network effect established by current usage.

### The licensing and openness decision is real

The repository's public license is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, with additional grants and separate commercial terms in `COMMERCIAL.md`. Portable does not mean unrestricted commercial reuse, and “source available” should not be presented as an unrestricted open-source commercial grant.

A publisher linking to a hosted companion is a different arrangement from embedding, white-labeling, or operating Future Forge as part of a commercial product. Decide the actual rights for each pilot. Do not assume that a free reader experience makes every publisher arrangement non-commercial.

If broad third-party adoption becomes the goal, the owner will need to decide how the editorial format, authoring materials, runtime, hosting, branding, and commercial permissions fit together. This document recommends clarity, not a license change. A familiar branded convention is also possible without making the whole runtime an open standard.

## 9. The first experiment

### Select one source and one potential collaborator

Choose a piece with a specific, consequential claim, a manageable evidence base, and visible reader discussion. Prefer a creator who responds to readers and whose usual format can accommodate a companion. Audience fit and editorial receptivity matter more than headline follower count.

Do not select the creator solely because they are famous or assume existing engagement is permission. No named partner, placement, or audience commitment exists yet.

### Prepare a small, inspectable offer

Prepare one source-grounded challenge, a sketch of the corresponding Quest, and a clear account of what Sic will handle. Use text and existing materials first. Do not commission new art, build an embed, or complete a large Quest package before learning whether the creator wants the contribution.

Suggested outreach, adapted to the actual source:

> Your piece on [specific subject] left me thinking about [specific implication]. I drafted this reader challenge: [include the actual short challenge].
>
> I build Future Forge, where people learn about emerging technology by inventing with it. Would you be interested in using this as a follow-up to your piece? I can prepare it and respond to participants. The first activity can stay in your usual discussion space; no signup or application required for that part.
>
> If people want to develop their ideas further, we can offer a corresponding Future Forge Quest with its access conditions stated clearly. You can review the companion first; I would credit and link your original throughout.

### Agree the placement and limits before delivery

Agree where the invitation appears, what the partner approves, what attribution means, who responds, how long the pilot runs, what participant material may be quoted, and the access/cost arrangement. Ask permission for follow-up interviews or public examples separately. Do not scrape participant identities or turn participation into a mailing list.

For the first trial, set a founder-time ceiling and a hosted-usage ceiling before publication. Zero marketing budget means no purchased reach; it does not authorize additional infrastructure spend. If the deep experience cannot be offered within current capacity, state that and scope a supported session rather than promise unrestricted access.

### Observe the full path

| Stage | What to record | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Partner response | Whether the creator accepts, declines, or requests changes, and why. | An endorsement is not participant demand. |
| Publication | The exact placement URL and publication time; exposure only if genuinely available. | A published companion does not prove it was seen. |
| First contribution | Actual approaches, questions, or attempts—not likes alone. | A comment is not a Quest start. |
| Quest participation | Starts, access friction, meaningful inventing, assistance required, and outcomes where observable. | A completed guided session is not independent retention. |
| Learning | The participant's explanation of a capability, assumption, limit, or changed interpretation. | Self-reported enjoyment is not proof of improved judgement or learning transfer. |
| Repeat pull | A participant voluntarily tries another puzzle or a partner requests another publication. | A request is not sustained usage or willingness to pay. |
| Delivery cost | Founder time and observed operating usage. | Contributed labor is not a scalable margin. |

Use manual notes where instrumentation does not exist. Keep unknown exposure unknown; never compute a conversion rate with an invented denominator. Pre-agree what counts as a start or meaningful contribution so counts do not shift after seeing results.

**Decision rule:** One pilot can justify another experiment, not a market-fit claim. Continue when the observed response warrants the effort, revise the specific failed transition when it does not, and pause if delivery exceeds the agreed limits. Do not expand to a series because the partner was polite.

## 10. Existing foundation versus proposed work

This review checked repository documentation and the relevant Quest/catalog tests. It did not verify a live partner campaign, hosted checkout, or full participant access journey.

| Capability | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|
| Portable playable Quest content | The existing `future-forge.quest-tile/v1` document supports the scenario, spotlight technology, grounding, and optional learning content. “Puzzle” need not be a new schema kind. |
| Bundled delivery | `.ffquest` packages can carry Quest JSON, optional lesson pages, and assets. Packaging is not an embeddable publisher widget. |
| Sponsorship attribution | `sponsorName` and `sponsorBanner` provide text attribution. Sponsorship is intended not to alter invention scoring or prescribe a product. Catalog grouping and a billing helper do inspect sponsorship. |
| Source and author metadata | The documented `research` and `author` fields are stored but are not automatically presented to players; `research` is not the AI's evidence input. Visible attribution must be deliberately supplied, and capability truth belongs in `grounding`. |
| Access differences | Local Library content, remote learning content, explicit access values, and hosted conditions differ. Sponsor status does not establish an unrestricted no-account hosting promise. Verify the exact pilot path. |
| Partner-oriented delivery | The prepared companion, editorial agreement, placement, source disclosures, and repeatable production workflow are proposals. No automatic article-to-puzzle generator, publisher analytics, embed, partner portal, or revenue-share system is claimed to exist. |

No schema or access workaround should be introduced to make the pilot appear simpler. In particular, do not create fake sponsorship to select a catalog shelf or bypass a billing path.

## 11. Decisions recorded and decisions still open

### Recorded direction

- The user endorsed documenting and evaluating the editorial-companion idea.
- The ambition extends beyond breakthroughs to technology news, ideas, opinions, and forecasts.
- The existing Sponsored Quest feature is a foundation and comparison point, not a reason to label every editorial companion as sponsored.
- Current users, demand, distribution effectiveness, and customer pains remain unvalidated.
- No-budget, online-first constraints apply; existing X participation and occasional local attendance are inputs, not a new generic campaign prescription.

### Still open

- The first source, partner, and specific puzzle.
- Whether the initial activity is asynchronous, part of an existing discussion, or a supported live session.
- The actual participant access arrangement and operating limits.
- Commercial permissions for a particular publisher relationship.
- Whether repeated demand supports contributed editorial work, paid commissioning, sponsorship-funded access, a reader-paid offer, or some combination.
- Whether broader adoption eventually warrants product, attribution, authoring, or licensing changes.

This proposal records a newer strategic direction than parts of `marketing-plan.md`. It does not silently rewrite that plan or treat its earlier public-invent cadence as a prerequisite. Reconcile the plan after choosing the first experiment; do not maintain two contradictory execution plans indefinitely.
