# Food Security

## Blight takes the parish potatoes

**Place:** Ladder Ridge Parish

**Stakeholder:** Elena, parish school-kitchen lead

_Stats: 179 words · 14 sentences · avg 12.8 w/sent · lengths [17, 7, 5, 9, 12, 17, 7, 28, 7, 11, 17, 6, 19, 17] · craft gate: ok_

Elena opens the school kitchen before first bell and finds the potato bins soft at the edges. She presses a thumb into one tuber. It gives like wet paper. Lunch for eighty children was built on this crop. The parish fields above the ridge still look green from the road. Walk the rows and the leaves show the same dark freckles that ruined last year’s seed lot. Elena calls the co-op for clean seed. The clerk reads the same contract her neighbors signed in spring: buy certified stock on credit, repay at harvest, forfeit next year’s claim if the weight falls short. Blight spreads faster than the repayment calendar. Families already stretch porridge with water so the youngest eat first. Elena scrapes what she can into the pot and marks three names who will go home light. The driver is not only fungus. It is a seed market that sells one fragile variety on debt and calls the loss a farmer’s risk. Who redesigns seed and credit so a parish kitchen does not fail when the ridge turns soft?

## Dawn crates rot at the gate

**Place:** Copper Gate Wholesale

**Stakeholder:** Jamal, stallholders union runner

_Stats: 180 words · 16 sentences · avg 11.3 w/sent · lengths [13, 8, 19, 6, 11, 5, 13, 4, 2, 4, 18, 7, 13, 11, 24, 22] · craft gate: ok_

Jamal counts crates at the wholesale gate while the sky is still gray. Half the tomatoes already weep through the cardboard. The night truck sat two hours at the weighbridge with no cold power and no place in the queue. Stallholders paid gate fees before sunrise. They need firm fruit by midmorning or the day is lost. Jamal radios the yard boss. The boss points at the schedule: long-haul loads with export stickers clear first. Local stalls clear last. Heat climbs. Flies find the juice. A vendor named Ruth tips a crate and watches good food become waste she still owes money on. The union can argue for shade cloth. It cannot move the rule that meters cold space to the biggest contracts. Hunger shows up three streets over as empty pans by noon. The system keeps producing spoilage because the yard prices time and chill for distant buyers, not for the people who sell within walking distance. What would it take to design a gate that keeps local food alive long enough to feed the city that grows it?

## Wells on the circuit turn to mud

**Place:** Thorn Well Circuit

**Stakeholder:** Nia, mobile nutrition aide

_Stats: 170 words · 15 sentences · avg 11.3 w/sent · lengths [14, 4, 11, 12, 11, 8, 13, 11, 14, 3, 17, 11, 12, 7, 22] · craft gate: ok_

Nia kneels at the third well on her nutrition circuit and lowers the bucket. It comes up thick. The child on her hip turns his face from the cup. Last month this stop still gave clear water for mixing therapeutic porridge. Today the pump coughs mud and the line of mothers thins. A new fence runs along the upper wash. The landholder diverted the seasonal flow into a private trough for export hay. Nia’s route was drawn when the commons still fed the wells. She marks the stop closed and walks an extra hour to the clinic cistern. Two sachets short. She chooses the smaller infants and sends older siblings home with advice that cannot fill a stomach. The harm is hunger that arrives as dust on the tongue. The driver is a water claim that treats village wells as leftover. Nia cannot treat dehydration with a pamphlet. Who designs rights and recharge so a mobile aide does not have to ration soft food because a fence took the flow?

## Barges pass the small jetties by

**Place:** Millrace Flats

**Stakeholder:** Oksana, co-op silo clerk

_Stats: 161 words · 15 sentences · avg 10.7 w/sent · lengths [16, 4, 14, 10, 6, 18, 6, 3, 13, 8, 5, 11, 15, 10, 22] · craft gate: ok_

Oksana stands on the co-op jetty with the ledger open as the grain barge holds mid-channel. It does not slow. The captain already radioed: contract loads for the upriver mill take the draft line. Millrace’s sack weight is too small to justify the stop. Oksana’s neighbors harvested on shared credit. The silo is dry enough, but the buyer who holds their notes pays only on delivered barge weight. Without a stop, the grain sits. Interest does not. She walks the quay and tells three families the payment window slipped again. One father cancels the order for school shoes. Another skips the clinic fee. The co-op can patch bags and pray for a smaller boat. It cannot rewrite the charter that sends full barges past short jetties toward distant mills. Hunger here begins as a boat that never ties up. Who redesigns river credit and calling rights so a flats co-op is not starved by traffic that treats their harvest as noise?

# Maternal Health

## Bamboo stretcher at the switchback

**Place:** Solukhumbu trail clinic

**Stakeholder:** Trail health volunteer circle

_Stats: 156 words · 12 sentences · avg 13.0 w/sent · lengths [11, 5, 7, 26, 6, 15, 25, 6, 15, 6, 17, 17] · craft gate: ok_

Pasang tightens the bamboo stretcher straps at the switchback below Namche. The mother’s breathing is shallow. Blood darkens the blanket at her hips. The nearest roadhead is still six hours of steep trail away, and the hired porter crew will not start until morning rates are paid in cash. Night wind cuts across the ridge. The trail clinic’s single oxytocin ampule sits in a cooler that lost ice at noon. Volunteer health workers radio the district hospital and hear the same reply they heard last month: the ambulance waits at the blacktop, not on footpaths. Porters price risk by the hour. Families sell a goat or borrow from a shopkeeper before anyone moves a laboring woman. The path keeps producing the delay. By the time the stretcher reaches gravel, Pasang’s cousin has already lost too much blood to stand. Who designs the first hour of emergency birth care when the road never comes to the door?

## Night heat on the birth floor

**Place:** Rakhiyal chawl maternity room

**Stakeholder:** Chawl women’s health sabha

_Stats: 120 words · 12 sentences · avg 10.0 w/sent · lengths [12, 9, 7, 6, 4, 19, 7, 12, 11, 5, 19, 9] · craft gate: ok_

Meena wipes her sister’s forehead with a cloth that is already warm. Night holds the day’s heat under the tin roof. Mothers share cots on the birth floor. When the grid dies, fans stop. The sterilizer goes cold. A new mother spikes a fever, and there is no clean way to cool her or keep instruments safe. Landlords still meter power by the room. Wiring was built for lights and phones, not for round-the-clock birth care. The sabha’s small inverter feeds a single bulb in the corridor. Backup never reaches this floor. Meena watches the thermometer climb and counts how many hours until dawn tariff rates make a private clinic thinkable. Who designs power for the hour a life arrives?

## Dust in the labor queue

**Place:** Cerro Alto workers’ maternity desk

**Stakeholder:** Mine spouses’ care committee

_Stats: 151 words · 12 sentences · avg 12.6 w/sent · lengths [15, 9, 13, 8, 4, 15, 14, 9, 16, 16, 18, 14] · craft gate: ok_

Rosa signs the clinic log with a hand that still shakes from the night’s headache. The mine spouses’ desk opens after first shift change. Dust from the haul road coats the plastic chairs in the labor queue. A nurse checks blood pressure twice and frowns. Rosa’s ankles are swollen. She needs observation now, not after her husband’s overtime card clears the company clinic fee. Shift rules treat a spouse visit as non-urgent unless a supervisor stamps the form. The stamp desk closes when the haul trucks roll. Women learn to hide early warning signs until a seizure or a bleed forces the gate. The care committee keeps a shared envelope for emergency fees, and it is almost empty again. Rosa’s name sits three lines down the list while the clock on the wall marks another unpaid hour. Who redesigns the gate so a rising blood pressure counts before the shift bell?

## Warm vials at low tide

**Place:** Mtwapa creek birth shelter

**Stakeholder:** Creek midwife cooperative

_Stats: 146 words · 11 sentences · avg 13.3 w/sent · lengths [16, 9, 18, 12, 9, 11, 15, 15, 12, 15, 14] · craft gate: ok_

Amina lifts the oxytocin carton from the clay pot and feels the warmth through the cardboard. Low tide has stranded the creek ferry on mud. The birth shelter’s solar lamp still glows, but the small fridge cycled off after clouds stacked at noon. A mother in second-stage labor bleeds more than the towels can hold. The cooperative’s protocol says give the injection within minutes. The vials have sat above safe temperature since the morning run. Mainland pharmacy stock moves on a schedule built for market boats, not for midnight hemorrhage. Midwives pool airtime to call a skiff that will not float until the water returns. Amina steadies the mother’s shoulders and watches the clock on her phone. The tide, the fridge, and the ferry timetable keep meeting in the same dangerous hour. # Sanitation

## Latrine queues send girls home by noon

**Place:** Sunwell Primary Compound

**Stakeholder:** Parent-teacher hygiene club

_Stats: 174 words · 17 sentences · avg 10.2 w/sent · lengths [14, 7, 14, 4, 9, 12, 1, 1, 12, 5, 12, 18, 22, 17, 6, 7, 13] · craft gate: ok_

Amina stands outside the girls’ block with her notebook pressed flat against her skirt. The morning bell has already rung twice. Ahead of her, twelve girls wait for two stalls whose doors no longer latch. The pits are high. Flies lift in a cloud when the wind shifts. She counts the minutes the way she counts sums on the board. Twenty. Thirty. Her younger sister tugs her sleeve and says she cannot hold it. They walk home before arithmetic. By noon the queue has thinned because half the class is gone. The parent-teacher hygiene club meets under the neem tree with a bucket of ash and a broken padlock. They scrape and lime and beg the ward office for a desludge truck that comes only when someone pays cash up front. The school’s budget line for sanitation is a single photocopied form that never clears the district desk. Enrollment for older girls keeps falling. Teachers mark empty desks and move on. Who builds a school day that does not end at the latrine door?

## Sewage owns the only stair out

**Place:** Ladder Cut Settlement

**Stakeholder:** Stair-block residents' union

_Stats: 160 words · 15 sentences · avg 10.7 w/sent · lengths [13, 12, 12, 18, 5, 4, 13, 20, 5, 5, 16, 5, 11, 10, 11] · craft gate: ok_

Rafi carries his mother down the only stair that still reaches the lane. Grey water sheets across the middle steps and turns the concrete slick. Last night the open channel above their block jumped its rim again. The landlord’s pipe from the upper tenements ends in a broken elbow that dumps straight into the cut. No one owns the elbow. Everyone owns the smell. Children with open sores on their ankles stop playing when the foam rises. The residents’ union strings planks and lays ash, then knocks on the estate office until the clerk closes the shutter. Repair is always next month. Rent is always this month. When the stair floods, elders miss clinic days and night-shift workers sleep in stairwells higher up. Rafi’s mother cannot climb alone. He misses another loading job waiting with her at the bottom. The channel keeps receiving what the buildings will not hold. Who redesigns the path out when waste claims the only stairs?

## Behind the fuel bay is the toilet

**Place:** Crossridge Freight Yard

**Stakeholder:** Night loaders' mutual aid circle

_Stats: 180 words · 13 sentences · avg 13.8 w/sent · lengths [19, 26, 5, 4, 11, 14, 26, 10, 11, 9, 6, 23, 16] · craft gate: ok_

Deepa finishes the midnight unload with diesel on her cuffs and a cramp she has ignored for two hours. The only toilets sit behind the fuel bay, past a chain the night supervisor keeps locked to stop “outsiders.” She waits while he finishes a call. The stall floors are wet. There is no soap. By dawn three loaders are bent over behind the container stack. Gut bugs move fast on a crew that shares one tap and one key. The mutual aid circle keeps a kit of salts and clean water in a toolbox, and they text each other which shift the lock might open. The yard lease spells out fuel, fencing, and truck counts. Sanitation is a side shed the tenant never budgeted to rebuild. When inspectors come, the chain disappears for a day. When they leave, the lock returns. Deepa either holds it, risks the dark lane outside the gate, or goes home sick and loses the shift bonus her rent needs. Who designs a freight night that does not trade a body for a bill of lading?

## The wing that smells before breakfast

**Place:** Olive Court Rest Home

**Stakeholder:** Family caregivers' council

_Stats: 170 words · 14 sentences · avg 12.1 w/sent · lengths [12, 5, 12, 1, 15, 9, 19, 25, 9, 7, 13, 8, 21, 14] · craft gate: ok_

Nurse Okonkwo opens the east wing doors before the tea trolley rolls. The air meets her first. A backed pipe under Room 12 has seeped into the baseboard again. Mr. Adel’s sheets are changed twice before breakfast, and still the rash along his hip spreads. Family caregivers arrive with extra wipes and quiet anger. The contract company cut night porters last quarter and stretched desludging to “as needed.” Needed is now every dawn. The council of families keeps a shared log of odors, fevers, and missed baths because the official chart only marks what fits the billing code. Staff wash what they can with what they have. Residents stop inviting grandchildren into that hall. One daughter cancels a visit rather than let her child remember the smell. Infection control is a poster by the lift. The drain line under the wing is older than the poster, and no one in the procurement chain owns its failure. Who designs care that does not ask elders to live beside a slow leak?
