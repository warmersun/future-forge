# Future Forge

Future Forge is an **inventing practice**. You take a global problem, land it in a concrete place with people who live the harm, and invent a **pathway** with emerging technologies on a hexagonal board. An AI co-inventor can teach, draft, and re-score the lights. You still lead. The invent gate is never “the model invents for you.”

This handbook is the map of the whole product: what it is for, the ideas that still govern it, what a session does, the features that ship, how to run it, and the agent skills that author its content. Deeper specs stay in their own files. The mission essay is [what-is-future-forge.md](what-is-future-forge.md). Install and environment tables stay in the [README](../README.md).

Player-facing copy calls this a practice, a session, a Quest, and Friends. It does not call Future Forge a game.

The ways people run it:

- [Self-hosting](#self-hosting) — `npm start` on your machine or a server you operate
- [Classes](#classes) — a shared Quest folder and a room, with an Education license when a school teaches with it
- [Learning quests](#learning-quests) — tutor, lesson pages, module progress
- [Sponsored quests](#sponsored-quests) — a named partner, invent still required
- [Multi-user sessions](#multi-user-sessions) — Friends rooms and hotseat
- [Agent skills](#agent-skills) — the four MIT packages that author Quests, predictions, trends, and collector cards

---

## What it is

People already hear about technology. Hearing is not the skill. The skill is connecting a capability that just became possible to a human problem, and saying how, here, this year.

Warmer Sun treats that connection as **the missing subject**: exponential progress in emerging technology reshapes every other field, and it rarely appears as general education. Future Forge is where that subject becomes practice — for a broad and young audience, earlier than a job funnel. Site: [warmersun.com](https://warmersun.com).

The working definition:

> **Technology is what takes something that used to be scarce and figures out a way to make it abundant.**

Heat, light, clothing, and speech count. Phones and AI are members of a larger map, not the map.

A session instantiates a global problem: a place, fictive people, crisis pressure, and a calendar. You invent for them. If you wait too long, the place can fall.

## Learning goals

The aim is a few habits of mind, not a certificate in one tool. The essay states them in full; this is the short list.

1. **Know what technology is.** Ordinary life is already tech. “Technology” is the craft of loosening scarcity, not a synonym for the headline tool of the year.
2. **Connect progress to global problems.** From a capability advance to a grand challenge — climate, health, energy, poverty, rights, long-term risk — and then to a pathway that can strengthen as those technologies improve.
3. **Hold the broad map.** AI is one family. The practice keeps eleven families visible so learners combine them. The tray below groups the playable cards into six domains.
4. **Know what emerging means.** Mature tools often improve slowly or plateau. Emerging technologies still ride a steep curve: better tools make better tools. Some domains look slow until a convergence unlocks them.
5. **Intercept the curve.** Too early is vapor: the tools cannot do the work yet. Too late and the place has already fallen. Timing is part of the invent.
6. **Practice convergence.** Hard problems cross families. Polymathy here is permission and skill to combine domains when the place needs it.
7. **Invent pathways.** A solution sounds finished on today’s tools. A pathway is a build with emerging tech so that, as those ingredients get better, the whole approach gets better. The long arc of scale is part of the idea.
8. **Treat ideas as the craft, and keep it voluntary.** Naming a mechanism, a beneficiary, a timing claim, and a tradeoff is the work. A brainstorm is a real start; it is not a shipped system, and it does not require a lab. There is no mandatory exam, so the practice has to be something people choose to continue. Ethics sit in the room with the invent (the four challengers), not after it.

Ray Kurzweil’s preface to *Danielle: Chronicles of a Superheroine* is the belief underneath: for the quandaries we face, an idea exists that lets us prevail, and when we find it we implement it. Future Forge is the practice of finding that idea under time pressure, with the map in view.

## Ideas that still govern the design

### Lineage: the 6Ps tabletop

Future Forge began in 2021 as a tabletop, **6Ps – Pun Intended** (© 2021 Tamas Simon / Warmer Sun Education). Historical notes: [6Ps Game - Pun Intended - Hex emTech.pdf](./6Ps%20Game%20-%20Pun%20Intended%20-%20Hex%20emTech.pdf).

| P | Meaning |
|---|---------|
| **Progress** | What is going on today in science and technology |
| **Prediction** | Milestones we estimate on future timelines |
| **Problem** | Something present to address now |
| **Prevention** | A future harm we still have time to prepare for |
| **Pathway** | What people invent — a build that can ride exponential improvement |
| **Pun** | A nod to Diamandis’s 6Ds of exponentials: digitized, deceptive, disruptive, demonetized, dematerialized, democratized |

The table’s job matches today’s practice: connect emerging tech to global problems, invent pathways, and share the stake — if the crisis goes unhandled, everyone loses. Impact was counted in Impact Dollars, after the idea that the way to make a million dollars is to impact a million people.

It was too early. The ideas were sound. The blockers were real.

### The contradiction

If the design hands people ready-made moves, they do not invent. A designer who scripts how the world will look, card by card, writes a lie and takes the learner’s work away.

If people truly brainstorm, another problem appears: who decides an idea is feasible? Early voting treated popularity as truth. Applause is not intercepting the curve. And without a map of categories, trends, and predictions, inventing collapsed into guesswork or silence.

The course **Progress and Predictions** ([101.warmersun.com](https://101.warmersun.com)) tried to teach the map first and offer the table as a reward. For a subject that is not mandatory, that funnel is a wall. People will not finish a pre-calculus of exponentials before they are allowed to invent.

### What AI unblocked

AI removed the blockers. It does not invent for the learner.

| Blocker | What changed |
|---------|----------------|
| People don’t know enough emerging tech | Learn on the fly: primers, the co-inventor, tutor modules |
| Hard to get help mid-invent | A partner that proposes; you still lead |
| Who judges feasible? | Feasibility and timing can be checked against capability, place, and the predictions bank |
| Course, then practice | Invent and learn in the same session |

No system gets to claim it knows the future. What can be asked is whether a claim is honest for today’s capability, and whether the pathway is built so the whole thing can get better as emerging tech improves.

### Problem first

The overwhelm for a newcomer is the long catalog, and not knowing how an idea relates to *this* problem. There is one Workshop for everyone. Disclosure collapses in place, per Quest, and reopens with one tap. There is no beginner mode and no prerequisite course.

The loop the screen is built around:

> **hurt → need → family → idea → tile**

The board’s red hexes are the problem. The tray answers “what could help here.” **For this place** leads with the Quest’s spotlight and suggested technologies, each with a one-line reason. The full catalog stays one tap away. A red crisis hex can point at up to three families and an **Invent with** action. Ask for ideas sees the live meters. How-it-works starts from the definition of technology: what is scarce here, and how does this family make it more abundant?

Detail: [problem-first-invent.md](problem-first-invent.md).

### Three perspectives

Unless a Quest turns meters off, the invention is judged from three angles:

| Perspective | Meter role | What you practice |
|-------------|------------|-------------------|
| **Here and now** | `local` | Does it help on the ground — the harm people feel today? |
| **Root cause** | `global` | Does it move the underlying problem, not only the surface? |
| **Support** | `support` | Can you earn goodwill and buy-in so the pathway can actually field? |

Local relief without a deeper lever is incomplete. A clever root-cause story with no support never leaves the notebook. Support without a real fix is theater. A law, ban, or cash transfer can be weather on the board — a rule already in force — and it can be the root cause in the story. The invent itself is still a capability pathway: something scarce becomes more abundant here.

### Words

Contributors drift when two docs use two names. Shipped player copy wins.

| Say to players | In code or older design notes |
|----------------|-------------------------------|
| Quest | mission (fine in data and file names) |

## A session

1. **Theme.** Choose a global problem. Themes live in `GLOBALS` in [js/data.js](../js/data.js): a few dozen problems on six shelves (health, climate and water, food and land, learning and work, safety and rights, long-term risks). A short “start here” row pins climate, water, infectious disease, education, energy access, and extreme poverty. Each theme is either a harm present now or a harm to prevent.
2. **Quest.** Read a short problem brief while Future Forge drafts several concrete places. Pick one. Solved Quests stay available. The browser caches scenarios in `localStorage`.
3. **Invent on the hex board.** The board starts with the crisis meters (a Quest may hide some). Pick an emerging technology, ask for ideas or write how it works, and mint an invention tile. Drag it on. **Bits** faces (blue, left) dock to bits. **Atoms** faces (pink, right) dock to atoms. Converters bridge both. A blue face against a pink face is refused: software does not grab a propeller. That refusal is the lesson.
4. **Lights.** Crisis meters and, later, challengers are traffic lights: red (hot or unanswered), yellow (strained), green (eased). Placing or lifting an invention re-evaluates neighboring lights. Looking at the board is the thinking. Each invention tile also carries a feasibility bar: is this claim honest this year?
5. **Hard questions.** When ready, summon the challengers onto the board — one by default, up to four: **Moloch** (system incentives), **Ethicist** (hard tradeoffs), **Stakeholder** (officials and community), **Mother Nature** (the physical world). Ease their lights with honest ideas.
6. **Hold or fall.** When every active light is yellow or green, declare the pathway holds. **Look Ahead** / **Wait +2 years** advances the calendar and raises crisis. The Quest fails if any meter hits 5 or the collapse year arrives.

**Learn** on a technology card opens a primer. The co-inventor brainstorms and tutors. You confirm anything that lands on the board.

A hex has six neighbors, so one invention cannot touch every given light. The cluster is the invent. A meter that is already yellow or green does not need a neighbor.

## Features

### Hex board and the emerging-tech tray

Everything you move is a hexagonal tile: inventions, crisis meters, and challenger concerns. The place and the named person are story, not tiles. Tiles do not rotate. Each hex is a picture under clear glass. Color meaning lives on the rim, the lamp, and the feasibility bar — green is a traffic light, never a “world.”

Worlds apply only to invention tiles:

| Border | World | Examples |
|--------|-------|----------|
| All six blue | bits | AI, networks, computing, gene sequencing |
| All six pink | atoms | drones, energy, robots, transport |
| Split — left blue, right pink | converter | IoT, 3D printing, materials, synthetic biology, VR |

Two hexes connect only when the faces that touch are the same world.

The catalog is **29** always-pickable cards in [js/data.js](../js/data.js) (`TECHS`), grouped into six tray domains (`DOMAINS`):

| Domain | What sits there |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Power** | Computing, energy, crypto, quantum, geothermal, tidal, solar, wind, wave, nuclear, batteries |
| **Automator** | Super intelligence, robots |
| **Mover** | Networks, transportation, self-driving, drones, quantum internet, space |
| **LifeForce** | Synthetic biology, brain-computer interfaces, genetic engineering, gene sequencing, alternative proteins |
| **Link** | Virtual reality (the VR/AR family) |
| **Portal** | 3D printing, Internet of Things, materials, nanotechnology |

That is the playable cut of the older eleven-family map: computing, clean energy, crypto, AI, robots, networks, transportation, VR/AR, IoT and sensors, fabrication and materials, and life tech. Finer cards (solar versus “energy”, sequencing versus synthetic biology) are how you actually invent. Each card has a capability line, a primer, use cases, risks, and synergy partners (`pairs`). `readyYear` is a soft horizon hint, not a lock.

**For this place** shows the Quest’s spotlight first, then up to four supporting technologies, each with a `suggestedWhy` sentence. **All emerging tech** is collapsed until you open it. State is per Quest.

Visual intent, including details that predate the shipped Workshop: [workshop-hex-invent-surface.md](workshop-hex-invent-surface.md).

### Attention, Budget, and Support

Three stocks sit beside the crisis meters. Crisis meters are the place. These three are the learner’s.

| Player label | Code | What it is |
|--------------|------|------------|
| **Attention** (AP) | `state.ap` | The action window. The first thinking ask of a turn costs 1. Further thinking that turn is free. Judging and submitting still cost Attention. |
| **Budget** | `state.budget` | Capital. It is a stock: ending the turn refills Attention, not Budget. |
| **Support** | `state.will` | Political will — goodwill you can spend. Older design notes say “political will.” The HUD says Support. A crisis meter may also be named Trust; that meter is the place, not this resource. |

**Two-act economy.** A local island this year should not also finish the root-cause meter. Later, more honest capabilities — after the calendar moves — are act two. When a scored pathway eases a crisis role, it pays **+1 Budget per newly eased role**, once per role this Quest. That payment funds act two. Authors tune starting meters and wallet with the economy lab so a Quest is **challenging**: solvable before meters go red, not trivial in year one. See [quest-economy-lab.md](quest-economy-lab.md).

### AI co-inventor

The Node server exposes `POST /api/co-invent` for scenarios, brainstorming, feasibility, neighbor lights, and challenges. Credentials stay on the server: a SuperGrok session (`grok login`, read from `~/.grok/auth.json`) or `FF_XAI_API_KEY`. The browser never sees the key.

If neither credential works, the UI still loads and a **local** heuristic partner answers. Live Grok reasoning and Imagine images wait until auth succeeds. Check the status line, or `GET /api/health`.

What the model is allowed to know depends on the call:

- **Grounding** on a Quest tile is capability truth (emerging tech, product category, capabilities, trends, a milestone, use cases, applications, honest limits). Fast paths read the first 3000 characters. Put honest limits early or they cannot turn a light red.
- **Player prose** (summary, brief, scene) tells the story and must not name the answer. The tray does that.
- **Tutor context** (`aiTutorContext`, spotlight advance fields) is for learning modules only. The tutor names the real advance after the learner has the story. Fast evaluators do not read it.

Apply buttons still confirm anything that would change the board. The co-inventor proposes; it does not write the pathway by itself.

### Timing, the predictions bank, and Look Ahead

Timing has two instruments.

**Capability trends** are measured curves: cost per genome, solar levelized cost, frontier training compute, and so on. Each file is one series with a compounding rule (doubling, halving, or compound annual growth), an anchor, and dated milestones. They draw on **Look Ahead** / Wait charts (log scale). The official catalog is published from Warmer Sun (`FF_TRENDS_REMOTE_URL`). A Quest may embed `trends` or `spotlightTrends`, which override the catalog by id. Schema: [capability-trend-schema.md](capability-trend-schema.md). Loading: [capability-trends-remote.md](capability-trends-remote.md).

**The predictions bank** is one JSON file, schema `future-forge.predictions/v1`, loaded at boot. The ship default is [predictions/default.json](../predictions/default.json). Set `FF_PREDICTIONS_FILE` to replace it (a path or an `https://` URL). A side-load replaces the default; it does not merge. The bank drives:

- the year dialog when you open the calendar, and the bulletin after the year advances
- outcome foresight cards (one milestone, one trend, one prediction still ahead)
- the **AI world clock**

For the claim-timing judge, rows already due (`year` ≤ the game year) count as demonstrated. The nearest rows still ahead are forecasts. That clock is **binding** on feasibility timing: it beats the model’s memory and web search. Quest grounding still wins if it explicitly contradicts a row. Idea sparks, the text co-inventor, the tutor, and the voice co-inventor see a shorter slice of the same clock and are asked to cite attribution when a forecast drives a call. Scoring, challenge posing, and scenario generation do not read it. Detail: [predictions-bank.md](predictions-bank.md).

Optional live web and X search (`FF_AI_SEARCH=1` or `--ai-search`) applies only to timing assessment and Ask for ideas. It is off by default.

### Future vision, briefing, and Read out loud

**Future vision** is an Imagine image of the place as the pathway takes hold. Stages run from today, to an early build, to scaling, to a new normal, as more technologies join the stack (`VISION_STAGES` in `js/data.js`). `POST /api/vision` generates those frames. The Quest’s scene line locks the locale.

**Briefing** walks `mission.briefMd` one paragraph per card (at most eight) before the full brief. Optional `briefBeats` tighten the captions or ship stills.

**Read out loud** uses xAI text-to-speech (`POST /api/tts`). The server caches audio by text and voice under `data/tts-cache/` so later listeners share one file. If the AI is offline and the line is not cached, the browser uses the device voice.

### Voice co-inventor

The waveform beside Send opens a Grok Voice session (`POST /api/co-invent-voice/session`). The browser talks to the game server over a WebSocket; the server proxies audio to xAI, so the API key stays off the page. There is no local-voice fallback. Voice needs a live SuperGrok session or `FF_XAI_API_KEY`, a microphone, and a secure context (`http://127.0.0.1` or HTTPS). The first ask of a turn still costs 1 Attention (free while tutoring). The call itself is not billed per utterance. A backgrounded tab with no audio hangs up after 90 seconds; an open mic runs until the default 10-minute cap. Portal does not serve voice.

## Multi-user sessions

Two ways to invent with other people. Both keep **personal invents** and **one shared crisis**. If the place falls, everybody loses. Standings among survivors matter when the pathway holds. You may race or help. The host starts the Quest. A round is one turn from every seat. Look Ahead on a single seat does not raise the shared meters; the table wrap advances the year.

Tutor mode stays off in both. A learning Quest played with other people is still an invent, without the step-by-step tutor.

**Friends** is a live room. From the title screen: **Create room**, then share the join address and the room code. Each person uses their own browser. The address friends open is `FF_JOIN_ORIGIN` (often a Tailscale Funnel host such as `https://….ts.net`). The design size is a small table, about 2–6 people. Share a lesson on the public hop `https://warmersun.com/forge/?q=<quest-id>`, which forwards into the game and starts that Quest. Post that link, not the raw Funnel host.

**Hotseat** is the same session on one machine. **Start hotseat** takes comma-separated seat names. When a turn ends, pass the device. The rules match Friends; the transport is the laptop in the middle of the table.

The long mechanics draft [game-and-multiplayer-design.md](game-and-multiplayer-design.md) is a design document. Its “current architecture” section describes an older loop. This handbook and the README describe what ships. That draft still holds the intent: personal invents, shared crisis, survive and then rank.

## Learning quests

A learning Quest teaches one recent advance and still asks the learner to invent. It is a Spotlight tile with tutor mode turned on.

Set `isLearningModule: true`. On the hosted catalog, a learning module **defaults to sign-in** (`access: "account"`) unless the tile says otherwise. The invent opens on the stepped briefing. The AI tutor sits on the co-inventor tab when the learner opens it. It teaches **one idea at a time**, in a short paragraph, for a high-school senior, and it does not dump the solution. After the learner has the story, the tutor names the real advance (`spotlight.advanceTitle`, `advanceSummary`, `asOf`): the family, where it sits on the curve, what just became possible, what is still ahead.

`aiTutorContext` is hidden curriculum for that tutor only: lesson goal, sequence, readings, illustrations, misconceptions, invent gate. Fast evaluators (timing, scoring, challenge) do not read it. Capability truth stays in `grounding`. Player prose still does not name the technology as the answer.

Progress on the device is **Learn · {module title} · Lesson X/Y**, from `lesson` and `totalLessons` (both required for the bar) and a shared `module` title. A multi-lesson path is a `kind: "module"` wrapper plus one full Quest file per lesson, same title and same `totalLessons`, lessons numbered in order. The catalog shows one module card. The engine does not lock lesson 2 behind lesson 1; the wrapper’s `lessons` list is the order. Completed lessons fill the segment bar from `localStorage`.

Lesson pages ship inside a **`.ffquest`** zip with the tiles: `lessons/<folder>/*.html` and illustrations. The tutor may offer one of those pages after it has explained the idea. Chat is the conversation. The pages are the textbook. Format: [quest-package.md](quest-package.md). Authoring: [skills/future-forge-quest/references/learning-and-sponsor.md](../skills/future-forge-quest/references/learning-and-sponsor.md).

Unsponsored learning paths appear in the **Learning** catalog, downloaded from Warmer Sun (`https://warmersun.com/quests/catalog.json`), not from the local `quests/` folder. A local copy or an import shows under **Library**, grouped the same way.

## Sponsored quests

A sponsored Quest names a partner and still requires an invent. Attribution is display-only: no score bonus, no forced tray, no sell mode in the co-inventor.

| Field | What the player sees |
|-------|----------------------|
| `sponsorName` | **Sponsored by {name}**, and the chip **Sponsored · {name}** |
| `sponsorBanner` | A plain-text tagline under that label. Not an image URL |

The job stays an outcome for the people in the place. Naming the brand is not the solution. The product may appear once, as the instance that hit a milestone, inside `grounding`. Unlocks and applications stay at the category level. Player-facing story, summary, and job do not require the product.

A sponsored learning path (wrapper or any lesson carries `sponsorName`) is listed under **Sponsored**, including when it is also a module. It is not listed a second time under Learning. Official Sponsored tiles come from the same Warmer Sun catalog as Learning. Refresh on the catalog skips the server’s ~10-minute cache.

`grounding` is required in practice. The skill’s copy rules and the one-lesson versus multi-lesson shapes are in [learning-and-sponsor.md](../skills/future-forge-quest/references/learning-and-sponsor.md).

## Other Quests

Every Quest is one crisis episode: a theme, then a local problem.

| Kind | Where it comes from |
|------|---------------------|
| **Shipped missions** | Hand-authored locals in `MISSIONS` inside `js/data.js`, plus scenarios the co-inventor drafts and the browser caches |
| **Library / External** | JSON tiles in the repo `quests/` folder (`FF_QUESTS_DIR` to override), or **Import Quest…** / a dropped file on the title screen. This is the classroom side-load |
| **Spotlight** | A real recent capability, written as a fictive place. The tray hints the spotlight and 2–4 partners. Player prose does not name the technology as the answer |

Spotlight pedagogy: research a real advance, write a fictive crisis where that capability is an honest fit, put the story in `briefMd` (the place, the bigger problem and its root cause, then an outcome-only job), and put capability truth in `grounding`.

Schema and field clips: [quest-tile-schema.md](quest-tile-schema.md). Folder and remote catalog: [quests/README.md](../quests/README.md).

A learning Quest that includes lesson pages ships as one **`.ffquest`** file: a zip of the tile (or a module wrapper plus its lessons) and the lesson HTML. The game still plays Quest JSON. The package is the envelope that moves from an authoring directory to warmersun.com. Format: [quest-package.md](quest-package.md).

```bash
npm run validate:quest -- path/to/quest.json --strict
npm run economy:quest -- path/to/quest.json
npm run pack:quest -- <srcdir> -o <id>.ffquest
```

`--strict` fails on craft warnings (technology named in player text, thin grounding, template leftovers, and the rest). The economy lab’s target verdict is **challenging**. Developer mode (`npm run start:developer` or `FF_DEVELOPER=1`) unlocks inspect chrome and the lab at `/tools/quest-economy`. Production `npm start` leaves developer mode off. The client reads the flag from `/api/health`; there is no URL override.

### Collector cards

A collector card is one real capability, in general terms, with no playable place on it. A learner opens a public link, collects the card into a library, and plays it as a **free reusable invention tile** on any board. The card’s technology is free when it is the only tile of that technology. Sign-in is required to use the collection.

Authoring produces `cards/<slug>.json` beside a 16:9 page image. Validate with `npm run validate:collector-card`. Publishing (`scripts/issue-collector-card.sh`) writes the card and prints `/card/<uuid>`; it needs the database and is only for when you mean to issue a link.

### Accounts, and the two processes

Sign-in is optional. Unsigned play is complete. Browser state (scenario cache, solved Quest ids, library imports, UI prefs) lives in `localStorage` until a learner signs in.

| Process | Command | Role |
|---------|---------|------|
| **game** | `npm start` → `server.mjs` | The practice: static app, co-inventor, vision, voice, Friends WebSockets. No Clerk, no Neon. |
| **portal** | `npm run portal` → `portal/server.mjs` | Warmer Sun Cloud HTTP APIs: Clerk, Neon, quest boards, webhooks. No game UI. Render runs this. |

Both default to port **8765**. Do not run them together. Override the game with `FF_PORT`. Render sets `PORT` for the portal.

Learner identity is a Clerk session. The Sign-in page is hosted at `https://cloud.warmersun.com/signin`. Local game never loads Clerk; the Sign in chip opens the portal and stores a session JWT. Set `FF_PORTAL_URL` on the game to the portal origin. After sign-in, Cloud can import the local solved set; cloud then becomes the source of truth for solved ids. Cloud stance and feature inventory: [warmer-sun-cloud.md](warmer-sun-cloud.md).

Two credentials are easy to mix up:

- **Clerk** is the learner account (portal only).
- **SuperGrok or `FF_XAI_API_KEY`** is the AI provider (game only).

Local play needs neither.

## Self-hosting

The practice is a Node process you run. `npm start` (`server.mjs`, port 8765) serves the app, the co-inventor, vision, voice, text-to-speech, and Friends on that machine. You do not need Warmer Sun Cloud, Clerk, or Neon. Unsigned play in the browser is the whole loop.

Put that same process on a server when other people should reach it. Point `FF_JOIN_ORIGIN` at the public URL (a Tailscale Funnel host is the usual Friends path). Your SuperGrok session or `FF_XAI_API_KEY` stays on that server. Side-load your own Quests (`FF_QUESTS_DIR`), predictions bank (`FF_PREDICTIONS_FILE`), and trend catalog (`FF_TRENDS_REMOTE_URL`). Set `FF_QUESTS_REMOTE_URL=off` when the room should see only the tiles you placed in `quests/`.

Two free grants sit beside the CC license ([COMMERCIAL.md](../COMMERCIAL.md)):

- **A meeting you join.** In person or online, you participate, and you do not charge for Future Forge. Friends, family, classmates, a club.
- **A server with no commercial intent.** You do not charge for access, seats, or hosting, and you do not market it as a paid or freemium product.

A school, university, bootcamp, or training organization self-hosting for enrolled learners needs a paid **Education** license. That case is [Classes](#classes). A company, client engagement, or paid multi-tenant host needs **Commercial** or **Cloud**. License text: [LICENSE.md](../LICENSE.md) and [COMMERCIAL.md](../COMMERCIAL.md).

`npm run portal` is Warmer Sun Cloud’s API process (Clerk, Neon, quest boards). Render runs that. It is not required to self-host the practice, and it does not serve the game UI. Do not bind game and portal to the same port.

Install, env files, and hardening stay in the [README](../README.md).

## Classes

A class is people plus a Quest folder on a self-hosted game. It is not a separate product mode, and the app does not ship a roster, a gradebook, or due dates.

1. Run the game (`npm start`, or the server from [Self-hosting](#self-hosting)).
2. Put validated Quest JSON in `quests/` (or `FF_QUESTS_DIR`). The server scans that folder at startup. Learners see those tiles as **External** Quests, first in the list, including in Friends. A gold External badge marks them.
3. Optionally turn remote Sponsored and Learning off with `FF_QUESTS_REMOTE_URL=off` so the room’s catalog is the folder you chose.
4. Invent together in a [multi-user session](#multi-user-sessions): a Friends room (each learner on their own laptop) or hotseat (one laptop, pass the device). Solo play against the same folder works too.
5. For a taught sequence, use [learning quests](#learning-quests): a module wrapper, lesson tiles, and a `.ffquest` of pages. The tutor runs in solo play. In a Friends room the same tiles are still the invent, without tutor mode.

**Import Quest…** on the title screen is the per-device alternative when you cannot put files on the server. The file lands in that browser’s Library.

A facilitator who is in the room and is not charging can host classmates under the free meeting grant. Institutional teaching — a school, university, bootcamp, or training org, including a multi-user class server — needs the **Education** license. That license is what covers self-hosting for enrolled learners. See [COMMERCIAL.md](../COMMERCIAL.md).

## Agent skills

Four MIT skill packages live in [skills/](../skills/). They teach **any** agent harness how to research and author portable JSON. They do not relicense the app. The app stays CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; only the skill trees and their examples are MIT. Each package works by reading its `SKILL.md`. A symlink into a harness skills directory is optional.

In this checkout, quest, predictions, and collector cards are also linked from `.grok/skills/` so agents here discover them. Capability trends are read from `skills/future-forge-trends/` directly.

| Skill | You author | It drives | Check |
|-------|------------|-----------|-------|
| [future-forge-quest](../skills/future-forge-quest/) | A Spotlight Quest tile. A learning Quest with pages also packs to one `.ffquest` | Library, External Quests, Sponsored and Learning catalogs, the briefing, grounding for every AI judgement, tutor mode | `npm run validate:quest -- <file> --strict` and `npm run economy:quest -- <file>` (verdict: **challenging**) |
| [future-forge-predictions](../skills/future-forge-predictions/) | One `future-forge.predictions/v1` bank of dated milestones, trends, and predictions, with attribution | Year dialog, outcome foresight cards, the AI world clock | `npm run validate:predictions -- <file>`, then `FF_PREDICTIONS_FILE=<file> npm start` |
| [future-forge-trends](../skills/future-forge-trends/) | One measured capability series (`future-forge.capability-trend/v1`): compounding, anchor, milestones. No invented statistics | Look Ahead charts; optional embed on a Quest | `npm run validate:trend -- <file>` |
| [future-forge-collector-cards](../skills/future-forge-collector-cards/) | One capability card plus a sibling page image | Public `/card/<id>` page; a free reusable invention tile once collected | `npm run validate:collector-card -- cards/<slug>.json` |

Quest hard rules worth knowing before you edit a tile: the spotlight id is index 0 of `mission.suggested`, with 2–4 supporting ids; player-facing prose never names those technologies or a product as the answer; `briefMd` runs The place → The bigger problem → Your job (outcome only); `grounding` holds the truth the model must respect; new meters default to local 3↑1, global 2↑1, support 2↑0. The skill’s `references/` spell the character clips, the prose craft, the lint codes, and the economy.

Predictions change how harshly timing is judged. Place years honestly. Due rows become “already real.” Rows still ahead stay forecasts. The shipped default mixes unattributed rows; `predictions/examples/musk-abundance.json` is the attributed example.

Trends are plottable facts: anchor and milestone values must be greater than zero (log scale), and status is `reached` or `predicted`.

Examples worth imitating:

- Predictions: `skills/future-forge-predictions/examples/musk-abundance.json`
- Trends: `skills/future-forge-trends/examples/` (sequencing cost, solar LCOE, frontier training compute, and others)
- Cards: `cards/examples/`

## Operations

Requirements: **Node.js 18+** (20.9+ if you enable Clerk). Full AI and vision need SuperGrok or an xAI key. Without them, static play and the local co-inventor still run.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/warmersun/future-forge.git
cd future-forge
npm install
npm start
```

Open **http://127.0.0.1:8765**. Copy `.env.example` to `.env` for the game, and `.env.portal.example` to `.env.portal` for the portal. Never commit either file.

Content knobs:

| Env | Effect |
|-----|--------|
| `FF_QUESTS_DIR` | Library folder of Quest JSON (default `quests/`) |
| `FF_QUESTS_REMOTE_URL` | Sponsored / Learning catalog. Unset prefers a local Warmer Sun checkout, then `https://warmersun.com/quests/catalog.json`. `off` disables remote |
| `FF_TRENDS_REMOTE_URL` | Capability-trend catalog. Same pattern; bundled fixtures are the offline fallback |
| `FF_PREDICTIONS_FILE` | Replace the predictions bank |
| `FF_PORTAL_URL` | Game → portal origin for Sign in |
| `FF_XAI_API_KEY` | xAI key when there is no SuperGrok session |
| `FF_XAI_MODEL` | Text model (the example env uses `grok-4.7`) |
| `FF_JOIN_ORIGIN` | Public URL friends open to join a room |
| `FF_DEVELOPER=1` | Developer inspect and the economy lab |
| `FF_AI_SEARCH=1` | Web and X search on timing and idea sparks |
| `FF_USAGE_ENABLED=1` | Token, image, TTS, and session rollups under `data/usage/` |

The process serves an allowlist of public assets (`index.html`, `css/`, client `js/`, `assets/`). It does not serve `.env`, `server.mjs`, or `data/` over HTTP. Expensive routes are rate-limited. `FF_API_SECRET`, `FF_ADMIN_TOKEN`, and `FF_TRUST_PROXY` are for a host you control; see the README before turning them on.

`GET /api/health` is the public co-inventor check. Detailed health and `GET /api/usage` are loopback-only unless you pass the admin token. Prompts and learner text are not stored in usage logs.

Deploy sketch: the practice is a long-running Node process. Warmer Sun Cloud on Render runs **portal** only (`render.yaml`): Clerk keys, `DATABASE_URL`, `FF_TRUST_PROXY=1`, no xAI key. People play on **game**, with `FF_PORTAL_URL` pointed at that service. Full steps, hardening, and voice limits are in the [README](../README.md).
