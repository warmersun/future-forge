# On the curve — public recording pack

One clip per teaching moment. Invent screen, first-person. Player-facing names only.

**Built pack:** [`output/on-the-curve/`](../output/on-the-curve/README.md) — `clips/*.mp4`, friend cut, full series.

**Series line:** Future Forge teaches that emerging tech gets better on a curve. You invent on that curve: too early is vapor, too late and the place has already fallen.

**Sting (optional, end of a clip):** *Invent on the curve — not too early, not too late.*

---

## How to shoot

- **One clip per item.** 15–25 seconds.
- Invent screen, first-person. Cursor visible. 1920×1080 if you can.
- Voice says the **Say** line. Do not say “feature,” “button,” “modal,” or “system.”
- Name what the player can click: **Look Ahead**, **Wait +2 years**, **End turn**, **Learn**, **Ask for ideas**, **Art of the possible**, **Timing check**, **Convergence**.
- Do not enable **Developer view**. Do not show JSON.
- Start the year at **2026**.
- End on the sting if you want a series closer.

**Setup Quest:** **Fever samples waiting at Crossing Clinic 7** (Spotlight · Gene sequencing). It brings its own chart. Walk the briefing (job, place, strain) then invent.

**Stack for charts:** Look Ahead plots families already on your stack. Pick **Gene sequencing** (and later a second family) *before* opening Look Ahead, or the charts stay empty.

**Friend’s short cut (the lesson, not the B-roll):** 1 → 8 → 10 → 11 → 5 → 14 → 16.

## 1. Look Ahead — how capabilities compound

**Teaches:** Stacked emerging tech is not frozen. Power, cost, or speed compounds (doubling, halving, or a yearly rate). Waiting two years moves you along that curve.

**Record:** Invent screen → **Look Ahead**. Show the log-scale charts, the **now** line and the **after Wait** line, then **Wait +2 years**.

**Say:** “Look Ahead is the exponential clock. You see how this stack gets better — then you decide if waiting is worth the crisis.”

---

## 2. Doubling, halving, CAGR

**Teaches:** Not every curve is the same. Some capabilities double on a period; some costs halve; some grow at a yearly rate.

**Record:** Same Look Ahead screen. Pause on a card’s compounding chip (e.g. **Halving · 1.5y**).

**Say:** “The chip names the curve. Halving cost is as exponential as doubling power.”

---

## 3. Reached vs predicted milestones

**Teaches:** Some marks on the curve already happened. Some are forecasts. Forecasts are not free unlocks.

**Record:** Look Ahead. Point at a green **Reached** milestone and a **Predicted** one.

**Say:** “Reached is history. Predicted is a labeled guess. You still invent for this year.”

---

## 4. Quest-owned trend charts

**Teaches:** A Quest can bring its own exponential series (gene-seq cost, battery packs, …) and spotlight which curve matters here.

**Record:** Open a Spotlight Quest (gene sequencing) → **Look Ahead**. Show the Quest’s own chart and the **Spotlight** badge.

**Say:** “This place doesn’t use a generic chart. The Quest brings the curve that actually matters.”

---

## 5. Wait vs End turn

**Teaches:** Time is not free. End turn is +1 year with no extra crisis. Wait is +2 years on the capability curve — and the crisis heats.

**Record:** HUD year. Click **End turn** (or show the help contrast **End turn vs Wait**), then **Look Ahead → Wait +2 years**. Show crisis meters rise.

**Say:** “You can sit still for a year, or jump the curve and pay in crisis. That’s intercepting exponential progress.”

---

## 6. Cards are never locked by year

**Teaches:** Emerging tech is always pickable. Honesty is about the *claim*, not hiding the tray until 2029.

**Record:** Open **Emerging tech** while the year is 2026. Pick a longer-horizon family. Look Ahead copy: *Wait is for claim timing, not unlocking cards.*

**Say:** “You can always pick the family. What you cannot do is pretend the 2034 version already works today.”

---

## 7. Year on the HUD — world clock

**Teaches:** Each calendar year, some capabilities shift from frontier toward near or now. Predictions stay forecasts.

**Record:** Click the **year** in the top bar. Read **World clock** / **capabilities shift**. Note: claims are judged against this year.

**Say:** “The year is not flavor. It’s the world your invention has to be honest in.”

---

## 8. Honesty bar on an invent tile

**Teaches:** AI scores whether *this mechanism* is possible or already demonstrated **this year**. Green / yellow / red. Same claim cannot quietly get worse just because you waited.

**Record:** Mint a tile. Show the bar under the name filling. Open the tile. Read the one-sentence reason. Optionally Wait and show the bar re-check.

**Say:** “Feasibility is timing. Is this real this year — or still science fiction treated as routine?”

---

## 9. Timing check with the co-inventor

**Teaches:** You can wait for cheaper/better world conditions, or rewrite the claim to fit now. Missing capability is named, not hidden.

**Record:** **AI co-inventor** → **Timing check**. Read wait-vs-revise advice.

**Say:** “The partner doesn’t lock the tech. It asks: wait for the curve, or tell an honest story for today?”

---

## 10. Convergence — two tiles catalyze each other

**Teaches:** Exponential progress often arrives as a loop: A makes B better, and more B pulls more A. That is not “two tools in a pile.”

**Record:** Place two matching-world invent hexes so they touch. Wait for the **Convergence** dialog (title names both fields; 2–3 sentences of A → B and B → demand for A). Show honesty bars tick up.

**Say:** “Batteries make better drones; more drones demand better batteries. Convergence is that loop — evaluated when the tiles touch.”

---

## 11. A pathway is one invent, not a pile of parts

**Teaches:** Connected tiles are scored as a system. The whole can ease a crisis, miss a root cause, or even make public support worse. The combination is the idea.

**Record:** Dock two or three invent tiles into an island. Watch **score chips** and crisis-light reasons (local / root cause / support).

**Say:** “The pathway is more than the sum of its parts. The AI judges the combination — what it eases, and what it still doesn’t.”

---

## 12. Combined honesty on the curve

**Teaches:** A stack’s chance of being honest this year is the *product* of its pieces. A weak link pulls the whole pathway down.

**Record:** Pathway panel **Timing** lamp. Two yellow tiles becoming a redder combined chance. Optional: dock **R&D** and show the multiplier.

**Say:** “On an exponential curve, the pathway is only as honest as the product of its claims — not the average.”

---

## 13. Traffic lights from the whole pathway

**Teaches:** A crisis or challenger light reads the *reachable* invent, this year. Touching the hex is not enough. A later tile in the chain can make an earlier one honest.

**Record:** Place an idea next to a red crisis hex. Watch the lamp and the reason. Lift the tile; the light goes hot again.

**Say:** “The light is not a sticker. It re-reads the pathway against this year and this place.”

---

## 14. Art of the possible

**Teaches:** For this stack and year: what works now, how it is advancing, labeled predictions, recent milestones, what that unlocks, what is still frontier.

**Record:** **AI co-inventor** → **Art of the possible**. Scroll the chain (now → trends → predictions → unlocks → near vs frontier).

**Say:** “This is capability literacy on demand. Not an encyclopedia — what is possible *here, this year*, and what is still stretch.”

---

## 15. Ask for ideas — honest for this year

**Teaches:** Sparks are local applications that could exist now, not 2040 slogans. Three different angles, same year.

**Record:** Pick an emerging-tech family → **Ask for ideas**. Show three tiles with art. Read one how-it-works line.

**Say:** “The ideas are already timed. If it isn’t honest this year, it shouldn’t mint as if it were.”

---

## 16. Learn — now, near, frontier

**Teaches:** Each family has a maturity story: what is routine, what is getting common, what is still frontier — plus a milestone, a trend, and a labeled prediction.

**Record:** Pick a tech → **Learn** (ideally while ideas generate). Show primer + **Now** / **Near** / **Frontier**.

**Say:** “Learn is the missing subject in a card: not ‘what is AI,’ but where this family sits on the curve.”

---

## 17. Spotlight Quest + capability story

**Teaches:** A recent real advance can ground the whole session. The AI treats that story as truth for timing and ideas — you still invent the local application, not the product name.

**Record:** Quest hub → a **Spotlight** / **Learning** Quest. Briefing walk (job, place, strain — no lecture). Invent with **Learn** / **Art of the possible** / honesty bar using that advance.

**Say:** “The Quest brings the curve. You invent what to do with it, in this place, this year.”

---

## 18. Outcome — foresight for your stack

**Teaches:** After the run, you see the exponential story of the families you actually used — not a generic tech recap.

**Record:** Finish a Quest (or a hold). Outcome: **Foresight for your stack** + **Technologies you used**.

**Say:** “You leave with the curves you practiced, not a certificate in one tool.”

## Do not say

These stay off the soundtrack and off any caption:

- Developer, JSON, overlay, schema, catalog id
- “Unlock the card,” “locked until 2029,” “wait to unlock”
- “Feature,” “modal,” “API,” “score function”
- Average of two tiles (the lesson is the **product**, not the mean)

Predicted milestones are forecasts. Do not play them as free unlocks.
