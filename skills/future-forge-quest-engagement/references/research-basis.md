# Research basis and evidence boundaries

This skill translates nonfiction on communication, learning design, and user capability, together with research on interest and game motivation, into quest-authoring criteria. The ten criteria, five-question gate, qualitative ratings, and test protocol are a design synthesis, not a published or psychometrically validated instrument. No source guarantees engagement for every audience.

## Books and practical nonfiction

### Chip Heath and Dan Heath — Made to Stick

Public introduction excerpt: https://www.smu.edu/~/media/Site/Provost/CTE/NFTE/Made%20to%20Stick%20ch1.ashx

Publisher/academic author listing: https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/books/made-stick-why-some-ideas-survive-others-die

The framework emphasizes simplicity, unexpectedness, concreteness, credibility, emotion, and stories. It distinguishes getting attention through surprise from maintaining interest through curiosity. Apply it to clear situations, human meaning, and questions worth exploring. Do not interpret the framework as evidence that emotional manipulation, decorative storytelling, or clickbait improves learning. Memorability and truth are different qualities; the book itself contrasts memorable false stories with truthful communication.

**Supports:** criteria 1, 2, 4, and communication choices across the review.

### Julie Dirksen — Design for How People Learn

Author's book page and chapter descriptions: https://usablelearning.com/the-books/design-for-how-people-learn/

The author distinguishes gaps in knowledge, skills, motivation, habits, and environment. The book covers learner perspective, attention, practice, and evaluation. Apply this by identifying the actual learner and bottleneck instead of assuming more explanation fixes low engagement. The public page establishes the book's scope; do not attribute detailed experimental results or unexamined chapter prescriptions to it.

**Supports:** audience discovery, criterion 7, evaluation, and the distinction between information delivery and meaningful participation.

### Kathy Sierra — Badass: Making Users Awesome

Publisher sampler: https://cdn.oreillystatic.com/oreilly/booksamplers/9781491919019_sampler.pdf

The sampler shifts focus from an impressive product to what people become able to do in a personally meaningful context. Apply this to the learner's capability and useful outcome, rather than technology admiration or mastery of the game interface for its own sake. This is a practical design argument, not a controlled evaluation of Future Forge.

**Supports:** criteria 5 and 9 and the learner-centered outcome frame.

### Richard E. Mayer — Multimedia Learning, Coherence Principle

Chapter summary: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/multimedia-learning/coherence-principle/4E80B70CB76E2166B76E5653EBDE7D3E

The coherence principle describes benefits of excluding extraneous material, including interesting but irrelevant words, images, and sounds. The summary reports transfer-test advantages for concise versions of the studied multimedia explanations. Apply it to relevance and cognitive load. Do not generalize it into “all stories are distracting” or “the shortest possible text is always best.” Human context that supports understanding or decisions is not automatically extraneous.

**Supports:** criterion 8 and removal of decorative rather than functional detail.

## Research on motivation and interest

### Harackiewicz, Smith, and Priniski — Interest Matters: The Importance of Promoting Interest in Education

Review: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5839644/

The accessible search-result text describes triggered and maintained situational interest and the possible development of more enduring individual interest. Novelty can trigger attention; meaningful, involving activity can help sustain it. Repeated supported engagement matters for development over time.

**Supports:** separating initial attraction, maintained participation, and voluntary re-engagement; criterion 10.

**Access boundary:** In the research underlying this skill, full-page retrieval encountered a browser challenge. Use the retrieved summary only for these broad distinctions. Retrieve the full article before adding detailed claims about interventions or effect sizes.

### Priniski, Hecht, and Harackiewicz — Making Learning Personally Meaningful: A New Framework for Relevance

Review: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6191053/

The accessible abstract describes a synthesis of relevance constructs across interest development, expectancy-value theory, and self-determination theory. It supports treating personal meaning as a substantive design concern, rather than merely announcing that material is useful.

**Supports:** criterion 3 and eliciting the learner's own connection.

**Access boundary:** Full-page retrieval encountered a browser challenge. The skill's particular bridge categories are authoring suggestions, not a quoted taxonomy or a claim that the review tested each bridge. Retrieve the article before making detailed claims beyond the abstract.

### Ryan, Rigby, and Przybylski — The Motivational Pull of Video Games: A Self-Determination Theory Approach

Full paper: https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf

The paper reports four studies. Perceived autonomy and competence were associated with enjoyment and future-play preference; relatedness also contributed in the multiplayer study. It discusses intuitive controls, challenge, and feedback in relation to need satisfaction.

**Supports:** criteria 6 and 7, and looking beyond compulsory completion.

**Boundary:** These studies did not test Future Forge quests or validate this rubric. Relatedness findings in multiplayer settings do not prove that adding a fictional character creates the same effect. Report association and study scope accurately; do not promise causal effects or a universal retention increase.

## Rules for using this research

1. Present quest-specific recommendations as design translations, not direct experimental findings.
2. Keep interest, enjoyment, memorability, learning, transfer, and return behavior distinct.
3. Do not invent weights, pass percentages, optimal durations, or engagement scores.
4. Preserve established capability versus scenario assumption boundaries in examples.
5. Use learner observation to test authoring hypotheses; never manufacture test results.
6. Retrieve and register new supporting sources when extending factual claims. These notes preserve the basis of the skill, not a substitute for reading sources beyond the scope summarized here.
